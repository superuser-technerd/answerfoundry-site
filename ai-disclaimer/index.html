AI Visibility Disclaimer

This page explains, without hedging, what AI visibility work can do, what it cannot do, and what we will never promise you.

Effective date: August 8, 2026

Last updated: August 8, 2026

This disclaimer is incorporated into our Terms of Service.

1. The short version

AI answer engines are third-party systems we do not own, control, or have any relationship with. We cannot guarantee that your business will appear in any AI answer, at any position, at any time — and neither can anyone else. What we can do is measure where you stand, fix the signals that are actually within your control, and report honestly on what changes.

2. What we actually do

Our work is measurement, diagnosis, and improvement of eligibility. Specifically:

  • Testing. We ask AI answer engines the questions your customers ask, and we record what comes back.
  • Diagnosis. We identify the sources and signals feeding those answers — your website structure and content, your structured data, your Google Business Profile, your directory listings, your reviews, and third-party mentions of you.
  • Improvement. We fix and strengthen those signals: technical corrections, schema markup, entity consistency, citation development, and content written to be quoted accurately.
  • Monitoring. We re-run the same tests on a regular cadence and report the movement.

That is the whole of it. Everything else follows from those four things or is not something we do.

The APIs, not the phone apps. This is the distinction that matters most, so we put it in §3 of every report as well as here. Where an engine is reached through its API, that API draws on the same retrieval substrate and exposes the same citation behaviour — which is what an audit reasons about — but it carries no session history, no personalisation, and none of the interface layer those products test on real users. Treat such results as evidence about the retrieval layer, not as a screenshot of what one specific person on one specific phone would see.

Not every engine on the list above is reached the same way. Your report states which is which, per row, before you read a single finding — and where an engine was not queried at all, it says that too, with the reason. A row marked “Not tested” is not an omission we hoped you would miss.

We could describe all of this more loosely and you would probably never know. We would rather you could check.

Every run is logged, favourable or not. A query where your business does not appear is recorded with the same care as one where it wins, and runs that fail outright are listed as errors rather than quietly dropped. The prompt set is fixed before any result is seen, so nothing can be added or removed to flatter the outcome. That log is the deliverable; the commentary is just reading it.

What a single automated cycle cannot do. Claiming and reading each third-party directory profile by hand — the work that finds four different suite numbers for one address in public circulation — is not automatable. It is part of ongoing engagements, not of a one-time audit, and every report says so in the section where it would otherwise be missed.

One cycle is a snapshot on one day. The same prompt run twice, minutes apart, can differ. The first genuinely meaningful comparison is the 90-day re-run, and it will include the results that did not move.

5. About the measurements themselves

Honesty about method matters more here than in most fields, because the underlying systems are non-deterministic.

  • AI answers vary. The same question asked twice can produce different answers, and answers differ by account, location, model version, and time. Our results are a sample, not a census.
  • Our scores are ours. Any visibility score, percentage, or grade we produce is AnswerFoundry's own composite measure using our own methodology. It is not an industry standard, is not issued or endorsed by any AI provider, and is not comparable to another vendor's score.
  • Snapshots are limited by design. The free Answer Snapshot uses a handful of representative test questions at one point in time. It is a preliminary read, not a diagnostic, and gaps it does not surface may still exist.
  • Prompt sets are a choice. We choose which questions to test based on your market. A different question set would produce different results. We will tell you what we tested if you ask.
  • Correlation is not attribution. If your visibility improves, we will show you what we changed and when — but neither we nor anyone else can prove that a specific fix caused a specific AI system to behave differently.

To test what AI systems say about you, we type information about your business — name, website, service, city or service area, and sometimes a competitor name or a customer question — into third-party AI products including ChatGPT, Gemini, Bing Copilot, Perplexity, and Claude. Those submissions are governed by those providers' terms and privacy policies, which we do not control. We submit business information only, never personal information about you or your customers.
