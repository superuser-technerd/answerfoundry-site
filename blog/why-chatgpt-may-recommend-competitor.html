Why ChatGPT May Recommend Your Competitor Instead of You

If you've asked ChatGPT a question like "best [your service] near [your city]" and watched it confidently name a competitor instead of you, the honest answer is usually not that the AI dislikes your business or that something is broken. It's almost always one or more of five specific, fixable issues: your competitor has more consistent mentions across the web, their website is easier for an AI to summarize confidently, your business information is inconsistent across listings, your content doesn't directly answer the exact question being asked, or there's no recent authoritative source backing up basic facts about you. None of these require guessing — each one can be diagnosed directly.

Below is a walk-through of each, along with a short illustrative example to make the pattern concrete.

Reason 1: Your competitor has more consistent citations across the web

AI systems tend to favor businesses that are mentioned consistently across multiple credible sources — review platforms, local directories, news coverage, industry associations — over businesses that are mainly described on their own website. This kind of external mention is sometimes called a citation. It doesn't have to include a link; simply being named accurately and consistently in enough places builds a pattern the AI can recognize and trust.

If your competitor has been actively collecting reviews, appearing in local "best of" roundups, and maintaining an accurate directory presence for years, while your citations are sparse, outdated, or inconsistent, the AI has more independently-confirmed information to draw on for them than for you. That's not a judgment about quality of service — it's a reflection of how much verifiable information exists.

Reason 2: Their website is structured in a way that's easier for an AI to summarize confidently

AI systems tend to avoid stating something they can't clearly support with source text. A website that plainly states "We offer X service, for Y type of patient/customer, priced around Z" gives an AI something direct to work with. A website that only describes services through vague brand language, image-heavy pages, or PDFs the AI can't easily parse gives it much less to work with — even if the underlying service is excellent.

If a competitor's site has clear, plainly written service pages with specific details (conditions treated, service areas, what's included), while your equivalent page is a single paragraph of marketing copy, the AI has an easier time confidently summarizing and naming the competitor.

Reason 3: Your business information is inconsistent across directories and listings

This is one of the most common and most fixable problems. If your business name, address, phone number, or hours differ even slightly between your website, your Google Business Profile, and directories like Yelp or industry-specific listing sites, that inconsistency creates doubt for an AI trying to confirm who you are. A related concept is entity consistency — the idea that all the scattered mentions of your business online should describe the same, single, coherent "entity." When they don't, some AI systems will simply avoid naming the business with confidence, or worse, repeat outdated information (an old address, a discontinued service, a former name).

Reason 4: Your content doesn't directly answer the exact question customers are asking

Customers often type or speak very specific questions: "which orthodontist in [city] treats adult patients with severe crowding," not "orthodontist services." If your website only addresses the general topic and never the specific angle a customer is asking about, an AI system may find a competitor's page that happens to address that specific angle more directly — even if your practice is equally, or more, qualified to help. This is a content-coverage gap, not a quality gap, and it's one of the more straightforward issues to close once identified.

Reason 5: No recent, authoritative source mentions you

AI systems appear to weigh recency in similar ways to how search engines have long favored fresh, updated content. A business with no new reviews, no recent press or directory updates, and no recent activity that a third party has documented may look, from the AI's vantage point, less current or less active than a competitor with a steady trickle of recent mentions — even if the business itself hasn't slowed down at all.

An illustrative example

Consider a hypothetical family-owned med spa in Winter Park, Florida — we'll call it "Willow Aesthetics" for illustration only; this is not a real client or business. Willow has been open for eight years, has loyal repeat clients, and does excellent work. When the owner types "best med spa in Winter Park for first-time Botox patients" into ChatGPT, a competitor two miles away gets named instead, with Willow left out entirely.

A quick diagnostic look might reveal a familiar pattern: Willow's Google Business Profile lists an old suite number from before a 2022 office move, its website describes services in broad spa-brand language ("rejuvenate your glow") rather than specifics like which providers are licensed to inject, what a first Botox visit involves, or typical pricing ranges, and its directory listings on two review platforms still use an old business name from before a rebrand. Meanwhile, the competitor's address is consistent everywhere, their site has a plainly written "first-time Botox visit" page, and they've collected reviews steadily over the past year.

None of that means the competitor gives better care. It means the AI had more consistent, specific, recently-confirmed information to work with — and, lacking that from Willow, made a confident recommendation with the information available. What to do with this

If you've run this same test and seen a competitor named instead of you, the fix isn't to try to manipulate what the AI says — that's not something any agency can honestly promise, and claims to "guarantee" a specific AI recommendation should be treated skeptically. The realistic path is to work through the five issues above in order: clean up inconsistent listings, make your website's content more specific and directly answer real customer questions, and build a steady, honest base of third-party mentions over time.
