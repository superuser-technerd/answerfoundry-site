Does voice search optimization still matter now that AI assistants answer questions?

Yes, but not as a separate project. The voice assistants people talk to are being rebuilt on the same generative AI that powers chat assistants, so most of what used to be called voice search optimization has merged into AI visibility. The voice-only tactics, speakable markup in particular, were always narrower than the advice around them suggested.

What this means

For most of the last decade, "voice search optimization" meant a specific checklist: write in question form, chase the featured snippet, add speakable markup, and hope a smart speaker read your sentence aloud. That checklist assumed a voice assistant was a thin layer over a search engine, picking one result and reading it.

That assumption is now out of date. In August 2025, Google announced Gemini for Home, a voice assistant built on its Gemini models that it said would replace Google Assistant on existing speakers and displays over time, with rigid commands giving way to more natural and complex requests (Kattukaran, 2025). Amazon had already introduced Alexa+ in February 2025, describing it as a next-generation assistant powered by generative AI and built on large language models (Clark, 2025). The assistant that answers out loud is now the same kind of system that answers in a chat window.

So the honest framing is this: voice is an interface, and AI is the engine behind it. Optimizing for the interface alone misses where the decision is made.

Who this applies to

This matters most for businesses that get asked about out loud: restaurants, home services, contractors, clinics, salons, auto repair, and other local services where someone says "find me a plumber" from the kitchen or the car. It matters less for businesses whose buyers research at a desk over days or weeks, though those buyers increasingly dictate questions to ChatGPT or Gemini on a phone, which is voice input into the same AI systems.

How we'd evaluate it

We wouldn't start with markup. We'd start by asking the assistants. Ask Alexa, Gemini, Siri, and ChatGPT's voice mode the question a customer would actually say, in a full spoken sentence, and write down what comes back: which businesses are named, whether your hours and phone number are right, and whether the assistant offers to book or call. A spoken answer usually names one or two businesses, so the gap between being included and being left out is wider than on a screen.

Then we'd check the sources behind those answers: your Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, the major directories, and any booking or marketplace service the assistant can act through.

Available options

  • Keep the old checklist. Question-style headings and concise answers still help, but as general answer-readiness, not as a voice-only tactic.
  • Add speakable markup. Reasonable for English-language news publishers. For most local businesses it is low priority, for reasons covered below.
  • Treat voice as part of AI visibility. Fix entity consistency, write direct answers, and get listed where assistants take action. This is the option we'd recommend for most businesses.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The benefit of folding voice into AI visibility is that one body of work serves typed and spoken questions at once. The limitation is measurement. Smart speakers and voice modes don't send a referral you can see in analytics, and none of these assistants publish a report of which businesses they named. You can test and log answers by hand, but you can't get a clean dashboard, and anyone who shows you a precise "voice ranking" is estimating.

Interest in the old term hasn't vanished, either. Semrush's U.S. keyword database showed roughly 2,900 monthly searches for "voice search optimization" when we checked on November 23, 2026. People are still looking for a voice-specific fix. We think most of them need a broader one.

What we know

Three things are documented by the platforms themselves.

Speakable markup is narrow. Schema.org defines the speakable property as a way to flag sections of a page that are especially appropriate for text-to-speech (Schema.org, n.d.). Google's documentation still labels its support as beta. It says Google Assistant uses speakable to answer topical news queries on smart speaker devices, that it works for U.S. users with Google Home devices set to English and for publishers of English content, and it suggests about 20 to 30 seconds of content per section, roughly two to three sentences (Google Search Central, n.d.). That is a news-reading feature, not a way to get a local business recommended.

Voice assistants are being rebuilt on generative models. Google's post describes Gemini for Home handling multi-part requests and open-ended conversation rather than fixed commands (Kattukaran, 2025). Amazon describes Alexa+ as built on large language models available through Amazon Bedrock (Clark, 2025).

Assistants now act through partner services. Amazon's announcement gives an example of Alexa+ using Thumbtack to find a service provider for an oven repair, and names OpenTable and Vagaro for reservations and appointments and Uber Eats and Grubhub for food delivery (Clark, 2025). For a local business, that means being present and accurate on the service an assistant books through can matter as much as what your own website says.

What we don't know: none of these companies publish how a voice assistant chooses which business to name. Anything more specific than the points above is inference, ours included.

Next steps

  • Ask three assistants your customer's question out loud and record exactly what each one says.
  • Confirm your name, address, phone number, and hours match across your website, Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, and Bing Places.
  • Put a two-to-three-sentence direct answer at the top of each service page.
  • Check whether you're listed, and accurate, on the booking and marketplace services relevant to your category.
  • Re-test monthly. Spoken answers shift as the underlying models and data change.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida has a large share of people asking questions on the move: visitors in rental cars asking for a restaurant or urgent care nearby, and residents on I-4 asking a phone to call a contractor. Those are spoken, local, right-now questions, and the answer is typically one business. Hours accuracy matters more than usual here, because seasonal and holiday schedules change often and an assistant that reads out the wrong closing time costs a visit you never knew you lost.

Frequently asked questions

Is voice search optimization dead?

No, but it has stopped being a separate discipline. The assistants people talk to now run on the same generative AI as the chat assistants people type into, so the work that helps one mostly helps the other: consistent business facts, direct answers on your pages, and listings on the services an assistant uses to act.

Should I add speakable schema to my business website?

For most local and professional-service businesses, no. Google's documentation still labels speakable as a beta feature and describes it as a way for Google Assistant to answer topical news queries on smart speakers, for U.S. users with Google Home devices set to English. It was built for news publishers, and it is not a general voice ranking signal.

Do I need different content for voice than for typed AI search?

Usually not. A short, plain-language answer near the top of a page works for both, because a spoken reply has room for roughly one answer. What matters more for voice is that facts like your hours, phone number, and service area are identical everywhere an assistant might look.

Can AnswerFoundry get my business recommended by Alexa or Gemini?

No one can guarantee that, including us. Alexa+, Gemini, Siri, and ChatGPT are third-party systems that choose their own sources and change without notice. What we can do is test what each one says about your business today, fix the signals within your control, and track how the answers move.
