Can I claim my business in ChatGPT, and should I?

Yes, if your business is eligible. On November 10, 2026, OpenAI opened a beta called Businesses in ChatGPT. Through it, a verified owner can claim a business profile, correct its details, and see how often ChatGPT mentions the business. For most businesses that qualify, claiming is worth doing. Just be clear about what it does: it gives ChatGPT accurate information to work from. It doesn't buy placement. OpenAI says ChatGPT keeps making independent recommendations and that claiming doesn't guarantee higher placement or sales (OpenAI, n.d.-a).

What this means

Until now, a business had no official way to tell ChatGPT anything. You could influence what it said only indirectly, through your website, your listings, and what other sites wrote about you. Business Hub, the dashboard at the center of the new program, changes that. Once you're verified, you can update key details about your business and, if you're a retailer, upload product feeds with prices and availability (OpenAI, 2026).

There are three parts to the program:

  • A claimed profile. When ChatGPT mentions your business, people can click through to your profile. A blue check marks profiles that have been claimed (OpenAI, n.d.-a).
  • Insights. Business Hub reports how many times ChatGPT mentioned you, how many website clicks it sent, and "discovery topics," the recurring themes of conversations where your site was cited. These numbers are aggregated, and OpenAI says it doesn't share users' conversations with businesses (OpenAI, 2026).
  • Business Agents. This is an optional chat assistant, labeled as yours, that answers customer questions using information and guidelines you supply. At launch it's open only to eligible U.S. ecommerce and online service businesses (OpenAI, 2026).

Who this applies to

Any eligible business located where ChatGPT is offered can apply. Eligibility depends on your industry, location, and OpenAI's verification and integrity checks, and you must own the business or be authorized to represent it. At first, claiming is limited to eligible businesses that have a website (OpenAI, n.d.-a).

The rules differ for local businesses. According to VentureBeat's reporting, local stores and restaurants can currently claim only a U.S. listing that already exists, and Business Agents aren't available to them yet (Franzen, 2026). A dental practice, a restaurant, or a roofing company can claim and correct its profile today. An online store or an online service business can go further and build an agent.

How we'd evaluate whether you're ready

Before you claim, check what ChatGPT already says about you. Ask it the questions a new customer would ask, then compare the answers with your real hours, services, location, and contact details. Any mismatch is something to correct once you have access, and it may also point to stale information somewhere on the web.

Then confirm you can get through verification. Reporting on the launch describes two steps. First, OpenAI verifies the claim itself through a business phone number, email address, website domain, or Shopify account, depending on the business. Then the person managing the listing completes a separate identity check through Persona before publishing changes, connecting a product feed, or viewing analytics (Franzen, 2026). If nobody on your team controls the business email or the domain, sort that out first.

Available options

  • Claim and correct only. This is the right move for most local service businesses. You get the blue check, accurate details, and access to the insights dashboard.
  • Claim and add a product feed. This applies to retailers. You need a verified identity before you can upload products, and the feed must follow OpenAI's product feed schema. Note that the feed doesn't complete purchases. Your live website still confirms the current price and availability when a customer acts (OpenAI, n.d.-b). Shopify merchants can connect their store instead of building a feed (OpenAI, 2026).
  • Claim and launch a Business Agent. This is for eligible U.S. ecommerce and online service businesses that are prepared to write, test, and maintain the answers the agent gives.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The clearest benefit is control over the facts. If ChatGPT has your hours wrong or describes a service you stopped offering, you now have a direct way to fix it. The insights are useful too. Most businesses currently have no idea how often an AI assistant mentions them, and Business Hub gives at least an aggregated count of mentions and clicks.

The limitations matter just as much. Claiming doesn't change how ChatGPT ranks or compares businesses. Your profile can appear whether or not you claim it (OpenAI, n.d.-a). The insights cover only what happens inside ChatGPT, so they tell you nothing about Google AI Overviews, Gemini, or Perplexity. And everything is in beta. Eligibility, features, and pricing could change. Digiday reported, as cited by VentureBeat, that claiming a profile and publishing an agent are free at launch, and that OpenAI left open the possibility of charging later (Franzen, 2026).

What we know

Here is what OpenAI's own materials confirm. The program launched in beta on November 10, 2026, and OpenAI describes ChatGPT as having 1.2 billion weekly users (OpenAI, 2026). Business Hub insights are aggregated and appear only once there are enough conversations to meet OpenAI's thresholds (OpenAI, 2026). Business Agent conversations are labeled, kept separate from ChatGPT's independent answers, and can be used as the destination for a ChatGPT ad, which is also labeled (OpenAI, 2026). For product discovery, ChatGPT can draw on the public web, private integrations, and merchant feeds (OpenAI, n.d.-b).

One reported detail matters a lot for local businesses: mentions are counted differently depending on the type of business. For an online company, ChatGPT has to reference its website for the mention to count. For a local business, appearing in a result is enough, even if the user never opens the listing (Franzen, 2026). So a local business may see a higher mention count than its click count would suggest.

Next steps

Write down what ChatGPT currently says about your business. Then check whether you're eligible and claim your profile through Business Hub. Correct anything that's wrong, and make sure the same facts appear on your website and your other listings. A correction in one place doesn't fix stale information elsewhere on the web. Once data starts coming in, review the dashboard monthly.

Orlando considerations

Most Central Florida businesses we work with are local service businesses: medical and dental practices, med spas, law firms, restaurants, and home services. For them, claiming the profile and correcting it is the realistic scope right now, since Business Agents launched for online businesses first. In a crowded market like Orlando, where several similar businesses often sit within a few miles of each other, accurate details on a claimed profile help ChatGPT tell you apart from competitors with similar names. But that profile works alongside your website, Google Business Profile, and reviews. It doesn't replace them.

Frequently asked questions

Does claiming my business help me rank higher in ChatGPT?

No. OpenAI says ChatGPT continues to make independent recommendations and that claiming doesn't guarantee higher placement or sales. What claiming changes is the accuracy of the information ChatGPT can use about you.

Will my business show up in ChatGPT if I don't claim it?

It can. OpenAI says ChatGPT may show information about a business whether or not the business has claimed its profile or built an agent. Claiming gives you a way to correct that information, not a switch that turns visibility on.

Can a local business create a Business Agent?

Not yet. At launch, Business Agents are limited to eligible U.S. ecommerce and online service businesses. OpenAI says it plans to expand to more business types over time, so local businesses can claim and correct their profile for now.

Can I see what customers asked ChatGPT about my business?

No. OpenAI says it doesn't share users' conversations or Business Agent chats with businesses. Business Hub shows aggregated counts and recurring topics, and only after activity passes minimum thresholds.
