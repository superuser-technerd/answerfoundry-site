Home / Blog / Turning off AI Overviews

Published October 18, 2026

If people can turn off Google’s AI Overviews, does AI visibility still matter?

Yes, it still matters. Google says AI Overviews are a core Search feature that can’t be turned off. People can skip them one search at a time with the “Web” filter, which shows plain text links, but there’s no permanent off switch (Google Search Help, n.d.). And anyone who avoids Google’s AI may still ask ChatGPT or Gemini instead. So a business still needs to be easy for AI systems to find, verify, and describe accurately.

What this means

“How do I turn off AI in Google search?” is one of the most-searched questions about AI search right now. Some people find AI summaries unhelpful, some don’t trust them, and some just want the old page of links back. If you own a business, it’s natural to wonder whether that pushback makes AI visibility a passing concern.

The short answer is no, for two reasons. First, Google doesn’t offer a setting that removes AI Overviews for good. Its own help page says “Features cannot be turned off” and points people to the Web filter instead, which “displays only text-based links without features like AI Overviews” (Google Search Help, n.d.). Second, Google is only one place people ask questions. A customer who opts out of Google’s AI one day may type the same question into a chatbot the next.

Who this applies to

This matters most for local and service businesses whose customers start with a question: “who’s the best dentist near me,” “do I need a permit for a fence,” “how much does a roof inspection cost.” Those are the kinds of questions that tend to get AI summaries and that people also take to chatbots. If most of your new customers come from referrals or a booking platform, the effect on you is smaller, but it’s rarely zero.

How we’d evaluate it

We look at your visibility in both modes. With AI on, we check whether Google’s AI Overviews and AI Mode mention your business for the questions your customers ask, and whether the details are right. With AI off (the Web filter, or a classic results page), we check where your pages rank in the plain list of links. Then we run the same questions through ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity. A business that only shows up in one of these places has a gap, and the pattern usually points to the cause.

Available options

  • Keep your classic SEO healthy. People who use the Web filter see a page of ordinary links, so traditional rankings still reach them.
  • Make your business easy for AI to verify. Consistent name, address, and phone details, a complete Google Business Profile, recent reviews, and clear service pages give AI systems something solid to summarize.
  • Write pages that answer real questions directly. A clear, specific answer near the top of a page is easier for both a summary and a searcher to use.
  • Check more than one engine. Monitoring Google, ChatGPT, and Gemini side by side shows whether you’re visible wherever your customers happen to ask.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

Working on both classic and AI visibility means you aren’t betting on which way searchers go. The tradeoff is effort: two kinds of results to watch instead of one. There are also real limits. Google hasn’t published how many people use the Web filter, so as far as we can find, nobody outside Google knows the actual size of the “AI off” audience. And AI Overviews, AI Mode, and chatbots are third-party systems that change without notice. Nobody can guarantee your business appears in them, and you should be skeptical of anyone who says they can.

What we know

AI Overviews can’t be switched off, only bypassed. Google’s help page calls AI Overviews “a core Google Search feature, like knowledge panels,” and the Web filter is the alternative it offers (Google Search Help, n.d.). Google launched that filter globally in May 2024. Search Liaison Danny Sullivan said it was for people who “prefer to just see links to web pages in their search results” (Southern, 2024).

AI summaries change how people click. In a Pew Research Center study of 900 U.S. adults’ actual browsing in March 2025, 18% of Google searches produced an AI summary. Users clicked a traditional result link in 8% of visits when a summary appeared, versus 15% when one didn’t. They clicked a link inside the summary in just 1% of visits, and they ended their browsing session more often after a page with a summary: 26% versus 16% (Chapekis & Lieb, 2025). The practical takeaway: when a summary appears, what it says about you may be all a customer sees.

AI search isn’t just Google. Pew’s 2026 survey found about half of U.S. adults now use AI chatbots, and about a quarter use them daily. 44% have used ChatGPT and 24% Gemini, and about four in ten adults say they use chatbots to search for information (Gottfried et al., 2026). Turning off one AI feature in one search engine doesn’t take someone out of AI search.

Next steps

Search for your main service and city twice: once normally, and once with the Web filter selected. Note whether your business appears in the AI Overview, and where it ranks in the plain links. Then ask ChatGPT or Gemini the same question. If you show up in one place but not the others, start there.

Orlando considerations

Orlando’s mix of long-time residents, newcomers, and seasonal visitors means a wide range of search habits. Some people will scroll past every AI summary, and others will ask a chatbot before they ever open Google. In competitive Central Florida categories like home services, dental, legal, and med spas, being visible in only one of those places leaves part of the market to a competitor.

Frequently asked questions

Can my customers permanently turn off AI Overviews?

Not through any Google setting. Google’s help page says AI Overviews are a core Search feature and can’t be turned off. People can choose the Web filter after each search to see only text-based links, but that’s a per-search choice, not a permanent switch.

Does the Web filter mean my regular Google rankings still matter?

Yes. The Web filter shows a page of classic text links, so a searcher who uses it sees traditional rankings. That’s one reason AI visibility work should sit on top of solid SEO rather than replace it.

If someone avoids Google’s AI, can they still find me through AI?

Often, yes. Pew Research Center’s 2026 survey found about half of U.S. adults use AI chatbots and roughly four in ten use them to search for information. Someone who skips AI Overviews may still ask ChatGPT, Gemini, or Copilot.

Should I worry that AI Overviews reduce clicks to my site?

It’s worth watching. Pew found users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits with an AI summary versus 15% without one. That makes being named and described accurately in the summary itself more important, not less.
