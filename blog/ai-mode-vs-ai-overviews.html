Published August 15, 2026

Do AI Overviews and AI Mode need different optimization strategies?

Short answer: treat them as related but separate channels. New research from Ahrefs found that Google's AI Mode and AI Overviews reach the same basic conclusion for a given question 86% of the time — but cite the same source only 13.7% of the time. Getting cited in one doesn't mean you'll get cited in the other, and if you're only checking one of the two, you're only seeing part of your actual AI visibility.

What this means

AI Overviews and AI Mode look like variations on the same feature — both are Google-built, both summarize an answer instead of showing a plain list of links, and both cite supporting pages. But Google's own documentation confirms they "may use different models and techniques, so the set of responses and links they show will vary" (Google Search Central, 2025). In practice, that's a meaningful gap, not a technicality. An Ahrefs study of 730,000 response pairs found the two features agree on the substance of an answer (86% semantic similarity) while pulling from almost entirely different source pools (Gavoyannis & Guan, 2025). That means a business can be confidently named in one experience and be completely absent from the other, for the exact same question.

Who this applies to

This matters most for businesses that already know they show up somewhere in Google's AI features and are trying to figure out how consistent that visibility actually is. If you've only ever tested the classic search results page with AI Overviews turned on, you've been looking at one of two increasingly distinct surfaces. It also matters for anyone budgeting content or PR effort against "getting cited by AI" as a single goal, since the research suggests that goal actually has two different scoreboards.

How we'd evaluate it

A proper check tests both surfaces separately, on the same set of real customer questions, rather than assuming one stands in for the other. That means running a query in classic Google Search with the AI Overview visible, then running the same query inside AI Mode, and recording who gets named, cited, or linked in each. It also means checking Google Search Console's Performance report, which breaks out AI Overviews and AI Mode as distinct segments under the "Web" search type — so the data to compare the two already exists for most sites, it just isn't usually being pulled apart (Google Search Central, 2025).

Available options

  • Manual spot-check. Free, and enough to tell you whether a real gap exists between the two surfaces for your most important questions.
  • Search Console segmentation. Pulls the AI Overviews and AI Mode performance data Google already collects on your site, broken apart instead of blended together.
  • Structured, ongoing tracking. Given that citation pools reportedly shift generation to generation even within the same feature (Gavoyannis & Guan, 2025), a single snapshot check is a starting point, not a conclusion.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The upside of understanding this split is that it stops you from over-reading a single good result. Getting named in an AI Overview once is encouraging, but it isn't evidence that AI Mode — which tends to produce longer, more heavily cited, more entity-dense answers — will do the same (Gavoyannis & Guan, 2025). The limitation is that neither Google nor any third-party tool can tell you in advance which sources a given query will draw from; both features are generated per-query using a "query fan-out" technique that issues multiple related searches before assembling a response (Google Search Central, 2025), so results can vary session to session even without any change on your end.

What we know

Google's official documentation states plainly that there are no additional technical requirements or special markup needed to appear in either AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond standard SEO fundamentals and technical indexability (Google Search Central, 2025). Independent of that, Ahrefs' analysis of September 2025 U.S. search data found citation overlap between the two features at just 13.7% overall, rising modestly to 16.3% when comparing only each feature's top three citations (Gavoyannis & Guan, 2025). The same study found AI Mode responses run roughly four times longer than AI Overviews and include about 3.3 named entities on average versus 1.3 for AI Overviews, and that when a brand does appear in an AI Overview, it also shows up in the matching AI Mode response 61% of the time (Gavoyannis & Guan, 2025). Search Engine Journal's coverage of the same report noted that both features can produce similarly worded, similarly reasoned answers while sourcing them almost independently of one another, which is the core reason a single-surface check isn't sufficient (Southern, 2025).

Next steps

Pick the two or three questions that matter most for your business — the ones a real customer would actually type or ask. Run each one through classic Google Search with AI Overviews visible, then run the identical question in AI Mode. Write down who gets named in each, not just whether you appear. If you show up in one but not the other, that's not a bug to panic over — it's the expected behavior these systems currently exhibit, and it tells you exactly where to focus next.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida's service-based categories — med spas, dental practices, law firms, home services — tend to generate exactly the kind of comparison-heavy, multi-option questions where AI Mode's longer, more exploratory answers show up, alongside the shorter AI Overview snapshot for the same search. A business that only checks the classic AI Overview result risks missing that it's either winning or losing the longer AI Mode conversation happening one click away, in a market dense enough that a missed slot rarely stays empty for long.

Frequently asked questions

Is AI Mode replacing AI Overviews?

No. Google's own documentation describes them as two separate features that may use different models and techniques, and says AI Overviews only appear when the system judges them additive to a classic search results page. AI Mode is a distinct, opt-in experience, not a planned replacement.

If my business is cited in AI Overviews, will it automatically show up in AI Mode too?

Not automatically, but the odds are in your favor. Ahrefs found only 13.7% of citations overlap between the two features, yet when a brand does appear in an AI Overview, there's a 61% chance it also appears in the corresponding AI Mode response, typically alongside more competitors.

Does this mean I need two completely separate content strategies?

Not two separate strategies so much as one broader one. Since both features reach similar conclusions through different sources, the safer approach is building comprehensive, well-sourced content and a wider footprint of corroborating mentions, rather than optimizing narrowly for a single citation slot.

How do I check whether I'm being cited in AI Mode specifically, versus AI Overviews?

Google Search Console reports both under the Performance report's Web search type, with separate breakdowns available for each feature. The fastest DIY check is still manual: run the same question through classic Google results with AI Overviews, then again inside AI Mode, and compare what comes back.
