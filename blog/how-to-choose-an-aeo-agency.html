How do I choose an AEO agency, and what should I ask before hiring one?

Ask any AEO agency four things: what exactly will you change, how will you measure it, what can't you promise, and where is the evidence behind your advice? Walk away from anyone who guarantees a spot in ChatGPT or Google's AI answers. Google's own hiring guidance tells business owners to find someone else when an SEO guarantees first place, and it now tells them to check AEO advice against its published documentation (Google Search Central, 2026a). One disclosure before you read further: we sell this service, so weigh this list accordingly.

What this means

AEO stands for answer engine optimization. SEO earns you a slot in a list of links. AEO aims to earn you a mention inside an AI-generated answer, where there may be no list at all. An AEO agency is a firm that says it can improve how often, and how accurately, those answers describe your business.

The tempting assumption is that this is a new discipline with its own secret rules. Google says otherwise about its own products: optimizing for generative AI search is, from its perspective, "still SEO" (Google Search Central, 2026c). That statement only covers Google, though. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and other AI answer engines run their own systems, which is why testing across engines still matters. A useful agency can explain both: the fundamentals that carry over, and what it checks engine by engine.

Who this applies to

This is for any owner who has been pitched AEO, GEO, or "AI SEO." First ask whether you need an agency at all. Google says a small local business can probably do much of the SEO work itself (Google Search Central, 2026a). Outside help makes more sense when you have several locations, a crowded category, an AI answer that gets your business wrong, or a site redesign coming up, and no one in-house with the time.

How we'd evaluate an agency

We've adapted Google's hiring checklist for AI search:

  1. What will you change, and where? Expect named pages, listings, and markup. Google warns against firms that are secretive or won't clearly explain their plans, and says you remain responsible for what a company you hire does (Google Search Central, 2026a).
  2. Which engines, which prompts, and how many runs? One screenshot proves very little. In a SparkToro study, the same list of brands came back fewer than 1 time in 100 (Fishkin, 2026). Ask how the agency handles that variation.
  3. Do you report rank in AI answers, or how often we're named? The same study found ranking position close to meaningless, while visibility percentage across many prompts and repeated runs looked like a reasonable measure (Fishkin, 2026).
  4. Will you use first-party data? Google offers a Generative AI performance report in Search Console (Google Search Central, 2026c), and Bing Webmaster Tools reports citations across Copilot and AI summaries in Bing (Microsoft Bing, 2026). For engines without a report like these, the agency has to show its own test log.
  5. What's the source for that recommendation? Google says good advice either labels itself as opinion based on data or experience, or cites official guidance (Google Search Central, 2026b). That's a fair standard for us too.
  6. Is your headline tactic something Google says to ignore? Google lists llms.txt files and "chunking" content among things you can ignore for Google Search (Google Search Central, 2026c). We publish an llms.txt file ourselves because other systems may read it, but it does nothing for Google Search, and it shouldn't be the core of what you're paying for.
  7. Can I see real work and call a past client? Google suggests asking for examples and checking references (Google Search Central, 2026a). Ask whether each case study is an independent client. Our own published case study is of a business owned by our founder, who has a financial interest in both companies. It's a methodology example, not an independent client result.
  8. What access do you need? For an audit, Google advises granting read access to Search Console only, not write access (Google Search Central, 2026a).

Available options

  • Do it yourself. Lowest cost, and the basics are public. It costs you time.
  • A one-time audit. Tells you what's wrong and in what order to fix it. Someone still has to do the fixing.
  • Monitoring only. Useful if you have people to act on what it finds.
  • Done-for-you implementation. Usually the costliest route, and where question 1 matters most.
  • Your current SEO firm. Often sensible for the Google side. Ask whether it tests other AI answer engines, and how.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

A good agency saves you time and brings a repeatable testing process. It can't control the outcome. AI engines are third-party systems, and third-party tools don't have access to Google's internal ranking data or guarantee performance (Google Search Central, 2026b). Credit is also hard to assign: when your visibility rises, an algorithm change or a new review may deserve some of it.

These should end the conversation:

  • A guarantee of placement, ranking, or citation in any AI answer
  • A claimed "special relationship" with Google or a "priority submit" (Google Search Central, 2026a)
  • A tool or service described as approved by Google, which doesn't evaluate third-party services (Google Search Central, 2026b)
  • Manufactured, inauthentic mentions as a strategy, which Google lists among tactics to ignore (Google Search Central, 2026c)
  • A cold email announcing that you're invisible, with no method shown (Google Search Central, 2026a)

What we know

Google's guidance here is direct and recent. Its hiring page and its third-party tools page were both last updated in June 2026, and its generative AI guide in July 2026. The first two address AEO and GEO services by name (Google Search Central, 2026a, 2026b). All three describe Google Search only.

The SparkToro data is more specific but has limits. Volunteers ran 12 prompts through ChatGPT, Claude, and Google's AI tools 2,961 times, and the work was done with an AI-tracking startup that has a commercial stake in the question (Fishkin, 2026). Its author called for larger samples. Bing likewise notes that its citation counts don't indicate ranking or placement within an answer (Microsoft Bing, 2026).

What we couldn't find is independent data comparing AEO agencies on results or price. Most guides on this topic, ours included, come from firms selling the service.

Next steps

Before you take any sales call, run the five-minute test yourself and save what comes back. That's your baseline. Then send the eight questions above to two or three firms and compare the written answers. Before signing, make sure the deliverables, reporting schedule, access level, and cancellation terms are in the contract.

Orlando considerations

Google suggests asking about experience in your city (Google Search Central, 2026a), and in Central Florida it's worth pressing for specifics. A contractor in Winter Park and a law firm downtown face different competitors and customer questions. Ask which local prompts the agency would test, and whether it checks by city and neighborhood or only runs a generic "best in Orlando" query.

Frequently asked questions

Is an AEO agency different from an SEO agency?

Sometimes only in name. Google's position is that optimizing for its generative AI features is still SEO, so a good SEO firm may already cover the Google side. The real difference to look for is whether the firm also tests how ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and other AI answer engines describe your business, and reports what it finds.

Can an AEO agency guarantee I'll show up in ChatGPT?

No, and you should be skeptical of anyone who guarantees placement in a system they don't control. An agency can commit to specific work, a measurement method, and a reporting schedule. It can't commit to what a third-party AI system will say.

How much should an AEO agency cost?

We didn't find an independent, reliable price benchmark for this category, so be cautious with any single figure you see quoted. Compare proposals by deliverables: what gets changed, how often it's tested, and who does the work. We publish our own prices on our pricing page so you can compare scope directly.

How long does AEO take to show results?

There's no standard timeline we can honestly give you, because AI systems update on their own schedules and answers vary from run to run. Ask each agency what it expects and by when, as Google suggests, then ask what it will report if that doesn't happen.
