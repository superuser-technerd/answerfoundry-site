What is a zero-click search, and what does it mean for my business?

A zero-click search is one that ends without the searcher clicking any result. The answer was on the results page: a phone number, a map listing, a featured snippet, or an AI-written summary. For a local business, that means a growing share of customers decide who to call before they ever reach a website. Traffic becomes a weaker measure of whether you’re being found, and what the results page says about you becomes more important. Nobody can guarantee what it will say.

What this means

It’s easy to assume a search either sends someone to your website or is lost. Most searches now do neither. SparkToro defines zero-click searches as “those that end without clicking on any of the results presented,” and its 2024 study estimated that 58.5% of US Google searches ended that way (Fishkin, 2024).

A zero-click search can still produce a customer. Someone who searches for a dentist, sees the hours and phone number in the map listing, and calls has completed a zero-click search. The business gained a patient and its website analytics recorded nothing. The reverse also happens. An AI summary can answer “who repairs pool pumps near me” by naming three companies, and if yours isn’t one of them, there was never a click to compete for.

Who this applies to

It applies most to businesses whose basic facts can be shown on a results page: hours, address, phone number, price range, star rating, and services. That covers most local and professional service businesses, including law firms, contractors, accounting practices, restaurants, and clinics. It matters less for businesses that sell through long research cycles or referrals, where a buyer will visit the site and read it closely no matter what the results page says.

How we’d evaluate it

We look at three things. First, Google Search Console: queries where impressions are steady or rising while clicks fall suggest people are getting their answer without visiting. Second, the Google Business Profile performance report, which counts the actions people took from the listing itself. Third, the answers themselves. We run the questions a customer would ask in Google and in several AI engines, then record whether the business is named and whether the details shown are correct. The first two say how much attention never reaches the website. The third says what those people saw.

Available options

  • Make the on-page facts correct. Hours, phone number, address, services, and categories on your Google Business Profile and major directories are what a zero-click searcher reads in place of your website.
  • Count actions as well as visits. Google’s Business Profile performance report counts calls as “the number of times a customer clicked on the call button,” along with direction requests, website clicks, messages, and bookings (Google Business Profile Help, n.d.).
  • Write pages that answer one question plainly. A page that states what you do, where, and for whom is easier for a search engine or AI engine to quote, and a quote can carry your name even when it carries no click.
  • Track mentions in AI answers. Check on a schedule whether AI engines name your business for the questions that matter to you, and which sources they cite.
  • Keep other channels working. Referrals, email, and repeat customers don’t depend on a results page.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

Accurate listings and clear pages help in ordinary search too, so the work holds its value if the results page changes again. The limitation is measurement. A phone call can be counted. Someone who read your name in an AI summary and called a week later usually can’t be. Search Console shows impressions and clicks, but it can’t show what an AI answer said about you. Being quoted on a results page also means handing over the answer without getting the visit, and that tradeoff is harder on businesses that earn from page views than on businesses that earn from phone calls.

What we know

The baseline was high before AI answers became common. SparkToro’s study used a Datos clickstream panel covering September 2022 to May 2024 and estimated that for every 1,000 US Google searches, 360 clicks went to the open web (Fishkin, 2024). That data is now more than two years old, and SparkToro itself flags the post as possibly out of date.

AI summaries appear to push the number higher. Pew Research Center analyzed the March 2025 browsing of 900 US adults. Users clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits to a results page with an AI summary, compared with 15% of visits to a page without one. They clicked a link inside the summary in 1% of visits. And they ended the browsing session on 26% of pages with an AI summary, compared with 16% of pages without one (Chapekis & Lieb, 2025). That’s one panel and one month.

Bain & Company surveyed 1,117 consumers in December 2024 and reported that about 80% rely on zero-click results in at least 40% of their searches, which it estimated reduces organic web traffic by 15% to 25% (Sommerfeld et al., 2025). Bain sells consulting services, and the traffic figure is its own estimate.

Google’s position is different. It says sites that appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in overall search traffic in Search Console’s Performance report, under the “Web” search type, and that it has seen clicks from results pages with AI Overviews be “higher quality” (Google Search Central, 2025). Google doesn’t publish data behind that statement, and Search Console doesn’t separate AI Overview clicks from other clicks, so a site owner can’t check it directly. None of these studies measured local service businesses specifically.

Next steps

Open Search Console and compare impressions with clicks over the past 12 months for your most important queries. Open your Business Profile performance report and write down calls, direction requests, and website clicks for the same period. Then search your main service and city in Google and in two or three AI engines, and note what a person would learn about your business without clicking anything. If the facts are wrong or you’re missing, fix that before you spend on more traffic.

Orlando considerations

Many Central Florida searches come from visitors and new residents who don’t know local businesses by name and are searching on a phone. Those searches tend to be the “near me” and “open now” kind, where the map listing or the AI answer settles the choice. The metro is also spread across many cities, and a results page will only show you for Winter Garden, Lake Nona, or Kissimmee if your listing and your pages say you serve them. For a business here, the practical question is whether that page shows the right hours, the right phone number, and the right service area.

Frequently asked questions

Is a zero-click search bad for my business?

Not always. If someone searches your business name, sees your phone number, and calls, that search produced a customer without a website visit. The searches that hurt are the ones where the answer on the results page names a competitor or leaves you out, because there is no click to win back afterward.

How do I measure zero-click searches for my own business?

You can’t count them directly, but you can see their outline. In Google Search Console, a query with many impressions and few clicks suggests people are getting what they need on the results page. In Google Business Profile, the performance report counts calls, direction requests, website clicks, messages, and bookings that came from your profile.

Do AI Overviews cause more zero-click searches?

The evidence points that way. A Pew Research Center analysis of March 2025 browsing data from 900 US adults found users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits to a results page with an AI summary, compared with 15% of visits to a page without one. That is one study of one month. Google says the clicks that do come from AI Overviews are higher quality, but it hasn’t published data to support that.

Can AnswerFoundry guarantee my business will appear in the answer when a search ends without a click?

No. Search engines and AI engines are third-party systems that nobody outside those companies controls. What we can do is check what those answers currently say about your business, find gaps in the information you control, and track whether the answers change over time.
