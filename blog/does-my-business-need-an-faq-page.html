Published August 16, 2026

Does my business need an FAQ page for AI visibility?

Yes — but not for the reason most people still think. Google formally dropped FAQ rich results from Search in May 2026 (Southern, 2026), so an FAQ page won't earn you that snippet anymore. That's a different question from whether FAQ content helps AI systems cite you, and the 2026 research on that second question says it still does.

What this means

Two separate things got conflated for years, and Google's own removal notice makes the split obvious now. A "rich result" is a visual feature in classic Google Search — the expandable question-and-answer box that used to appear under a search listing. Google restricted that feature to government and health sites in 2023, then removed it for everyone on May 7, 2026, with the rich-result report and API support following in June and August (Southern, 2026; Google Search Central, 2023). That's the part that's genuinely gone.

Citation is different. It's whether ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, or Perplexity actually pull your content into the answer they generate for a user. Google's 2023 announcement never addressed that behavior, and it still doesn't. The research that does address it — published well after the rich-result removal — keeps finding the same thing: FAQ-formatted content correlates with more AI citations, independent of whether it ever produced a rich snippet in classic search.

Who this applies to

Businesses whose customers ask specific, factual questions before they'll pick up the phone — what something costs, what's included, how long it takes, whether you serve a particular area. That's most law firms, home services companies, medical and dental practices, financial advisors, and other considered-purchase professional services. If your customers already know exactly who you are before they search, an FAQ page matters less; if they're comparing several unfamiliar options, it's one of the more direct levers you have.

How we'd evaluate it

An audit checks three things separately, because they fail independently. First, whether FAQ content exists at all and whether it answers real customer questions rather than generic filler. Second, whether FAQPage schema markup is present and correctly implemented — not to chase a rich result that no longer exists, but because the research below ties the markup itself to citation rates. Third, whether the section is labeled descriptively ("Frequently Asked Questions About [Service]") rather than a bare "FAQ" heading, since specificity is part of what these systems key on.

Available options

  • No FAQ section. Viable if your visibility strategy leans on strong reviews and consistent listings instead — but it's leaving a documented signal unused.
  • Plain FAQ text, no schema. Gives AI systems the Q&A format to extract from, without the explicit machine-readable labeling.
  • FAQ section plus FAQPage schema. The combination current research associates with the widest reach — citations linked across AI Overviews, Gemini, Google AI Mode, and Perplexity, not just one engine.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The benefit is real but bounded: this is correlational research on what already-cited pages have in common, not a controlled experiment proving an FAQ section causes any specific business to get cited. The Princeton-led GEO study is the closest thing to causal evidence in this space, and even it measured visibility gains from content optimization broadly — not FAQ schema in isolation (Aggarwal et al., 2024). No FAQ section, however well built, will produce a Google rich result again, and none of it guarantees a citation in any AI system. What it does is put your content in the shape these systems have been shown to extract from more often.

What we know

Google confirmed FAQ rich results stopped appearing in Search on May 7, 2026, with reporting tools being retired through August 2026, and said unused FAQ structured data "doesn't cause problems for Search" if left in place (Southern, 2026; Google Search Central, 2023). Separately, HubSpot's State of AEO 2026 research found FAQ sections correlate with more citations in AI Overviews, and that pairing them with schema extends the correlation to Gemini, Google AI Mode, and Perplexity — with descriptive H2 headings and questions formatted as H3s outperforming a bare "FAQ" label (HubSpot, 2026). The Princeton-led GEO study, the first controlled test of content optimization for generative engines, found that techniques like adding citations and statistics to content could lift visibility in AI-generated answers by up to 40% in its benchmark, establishing that structured, evidence-dense content measurably changes what these systems surface (Aggarwal et al., 2024).

Next steps

Pull the real questions your team already answers on calls and in email — not invented ones — and write direct answers to five or six of them. Add FAQPage schema. Use a descriptive section heading instead of a bare "FAQ." Then treat it the same as any other visibility signal: check back periodically rather than assuming it's fixed for good, since AI engines re-crawl and re-weigh sources on their own schedule.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida's density in categories like law, home services, and healthcare means several similarly positioned businesses often compete for the same AI-generated answer. Specific, locally-anchored FAQ content — actual service areas, actual pricing ranges, actual scheduling logistics — gives an AI system more to verify you against than a generic answer copied from a template, which matters more here than in a market with less overlap.

Frequently asked questions

If FAQ rich results are gone, why would FAQ schema still matter?

Rich results and AI citations are two different Google features. FAQ rich results were a visual snippet in classic search results, and Google removed that display entirely in May 2026. AI citation is a separate process — whether Google AI Overviews, Gemini, ChatGPT, or Perplexity extract and use your content when writing an answer. Google's own documentation only speaks to the first; current research on the second still links FAQ structure and schema to more citations.

Should I remove my FAQ schema now that rich results are gone?

Google has said unused structured data doesn't cause problems for Search, so there's no penalty for keeping it. Given the research linking FAQ schema to AI citations, removing it would be working against the evidence you have, not for it.

Isn't this the same as just writing good content?

Related, not identical. Good content can still bury the answer inside long paragraphs an AI system has to parse. An FAQ section forces a direct question paired with a direct answer, which is the exact shape research shows these systems extract most easily — a format advantage on top of whatever the content itself says.

Will adding an FAQ section guarantee I get cited by ChatGPT or Google AI?

No — and you should be skeptical of anyone who tells you otherwise. No one controls what an AI system chooses to cite, including AnswerFoundry. What the evidence supports is a correlation between FAQ structure and citation frequency, not a guarantee for any individual business.
