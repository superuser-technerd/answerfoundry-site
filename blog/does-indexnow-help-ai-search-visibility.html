Does IndexNow help my business show up in AI search?

IndexNow can help one specific part of the problem: getting new or changed pages noticed faster by Bing and the other search engines that support it, which matters for AI experiences built on those indexes, such as Microsoft Copilot. It does not make an AI engine more likely to recommend you, it does not reach Google, and it does not guarantee a page gets indexed at all. Think of it as a delivery notice, not a vote of confidence.

What this means

IndexNow is an open protocol that lets a website tell participating search engines, the moment it happens, that a URL was added, updated, or deleted (IndexNow.org, n.d.-a). Without it, a search engine finds out about changes on its own crawl schedule. With it, your site sends a short notice, and the engine that receives it shares that notice with every other engine in the protocol (IndexNow.org, 2024).

Why this comes up in AI search conversations: answer engines that pull live information from a search index can only use what that index holds. Microsoft's Bing team put it directly when writing about AI-powered search like Bing Copilot: freshness signals influence how quickly updates show up in both search results and AI-generated answers (Canel & Madhavan, 2025). If your hours, prices, or services change and the index still holds last month's page, an AI answer built on that index can repeat last month's information.

Who this applies to

IndexNow matters most for businesses whose important details change often: service menus, pricing, availability, seasonal hours, event dates, new locations, or a steady stream of new pages. Think contractors adding service-area pages, law firms publishing practice-area updates, real estate teams with changing listings, and healthcare or aesthetics practices updating services. If your site is five pages that change twice a year, IndexNow is still harmless and IndexNow.org recommends it even for small sites (IndexNow.org, n.d.-b), but it won't be what moves the needle for you.

How we'd evaluate it

We start with the basics before recommending anything new. Is the site already sending IndexNow notices? Many platforms do it quietly: IndexNow.org lists WordPress, Shopify, Wix, GoDaddy, Duda, Drupal, and Joomla! among systems with native integration or plugin support, and Cloudflare offers a built-in option (IndexNow.org, n.d.-b). Then we check the things IndexNow depends on: whether the XML sitemap exists, is referenced in robots.txt, and carries honest lastmod dates; whether Bing Webmaster Tools shows the sitemap being read; and whether the pages being submitted are actually indexable, with no stray noindex tags or robots.txt blocks.

Available options

  • Turn on your platform's built-in support. The lowest-effort path. Many site builders and SEO plugins handle the key file and submissions for you.
  • Use a CDN integration. If your site runs through Cloudflare, its IndexNow option can send notices without touching the site itself.
  • Set it up manually. Host a key file on your domain, then send a request to an IndexNow endpoint whenever a page changes. Up to 10,000 URLs can go in one request (IndexNow.org, n.d.-a). This is a developer task, but a small one.
  • Pair it with a clean sitemap. Both Bing and IndexNow.org describe these as complementary: IndexNow for fast, page-level notices, the sitemap for a complete inventory of your pages (Canel & Madhavan, 2025; IndexNow.org, n.d.-b).

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The benefit is speed of discovery on the engines that participate. When you fix wrong hours or add a new service, the change can be noticed without waiting for the next crawl. Setup is usually free and often a single toggle.

The limitations are real, and worth stating plainly:

  • It is a notice, not a request that must be honored. IndexNow.org says outright that submitting a URL does not guarantee immediate indexing, and each engine still decides whether to crawl and index it based on quality and its own crawl budget (IndexNow.org, n.d.-b).
  • Google is not in the protocol. As of September 27, 2026, the list of participating endpoints on IndexNow.org includes Bing, Yandex, Naver, Seznam.cz, Yep, and Amazon, but not Google (IndexNow.org, n.d.-b). For Google, the documented paths remain a sitemap submitted through robots.txt or Search Console. Google retired its old unauthenticated sitemap "ping" in 2023 and said accurate lastmod dates are what it uses to schedule recrawls (Illyes, 2023).
  • Indexing is not being recommended. Getting a page into an index is the entry ticket. Whether an AI answer names your business still depends on whether it can verify who you are, what you do, and that other sources agree.
  • Over-submitting wastes effort. IndexNow.org advises submitting only meaningful changes, not every cosmetic edit, and notes that submissions count toward a site's crawl quota (IndexNow.org, n.d.-b).

What we know

Here's what the primary sources actually say, as of this post's date:

  • Participating engines agree to share submitted URLs with all other participants, so one submission reaches the whole group (IndexNow.org, 2024).
  • Bing's product team recommends combining a complete XML sitemap, accurate lastmod values, and IndexNow for AI-powered search, and states that no tool can guarantee when or how content appears in AI-generated results (Canel & Madhavan, 2025).
  • Bing says it fetches a submitted sitemap right away and then typically revisits it at least once per day, and that it ignores the optional changefreq and priority tags (Canel & Madhavan, 2025).
  • Google says its sitemap ping endpoint stopped working after deprecation, that it does use lastmod to schedule crawls when the dates match reality, and that it does not use changefreq or priority (Illyes, 2023).

What nobody has published is a clean, independent measurement showing that sites using IndexNow get cited more often in AI answers. We haven't seen one, so we don't claim one. The honest case for IndexNow is about getting accurate, current pages into the indexes some AI tools draw from, faster.

Next steps

Check whether your platform already has IndexNow turned on before paying anyone to set it up. Then verify the sitemap: submitted in Bing Webmaster Tools and Google Search Console, referenced in robots.txt, with lastmod dates that change only when the content really does. If Bing matters to you for AI reasons, our post on whether you need Bing to show up in ChatGPT covers that side. Orlando considerations

Central Florida businesses change their public details more often than they might think: storm-related closures and adjusted hours during hurricane season, seasonal pricing tied to tourism, and new service areas as the region keeps growing. Each of those is exactly the kind of change you want search indexes to pick up quickly, because an AI answer that repeats stale hours sends a customer to a locked door. Accurate hours on your own site and listings come first (see does business hours accuracy affect AI visibility); IndexNow just helps the participating engines notice the update sooner.

Frequently asked questions

Does IndexNow work with Google?

No. As of September 27, 2026, Google is not listed as a participating search engine on IndexNow.org. For Google, submit your sitemap through Search Console or reference it in robots.txt, and keep lastmod dates accurate.

Will IndexNow get my business mentioned in ChatGPT or Copilot?

Not by itself. IndexNow can help participating engines notice new or changed pages faster. Whether an AI answer names your business depends on many other signals, and no one can guarantee placement in AI-generated answers.

Do I still need a sitemap if I use IndexNow?

Yes. Bing and IndexNow.org both describe them as complementary: IndexNow sends fast notices about individual pages, while the sitemap gives search engines a complete list of your pages.

Is IndexNow hard to set up?

Usually not. Many platforms, including WordPress, Shopify, Wix, and GoDaddy, support it natively or through plugins, and Cloudflare offers a built-in option. Manual setup means hosting a key file and sending a request when pages change.
