Published November 17, 2026

How do my products show up in ChatGPT shopping results?

ChatGPT shows products when a question sounds like shopping, and OpenAI says it picks them from structured product data (price, description, availability) that it gets from merchants directly and from third-party providers, weighed against what the shopper asked for. You can't pay for a spot in those results. What you can control is whether accurate, current data about your products reaches ChatGPT, and how strong your offer looks when it does.

What this means

When someone types "find me a waterproof hiking boot under $150" into ChatGPT, the answer can come back as product cards with images, prices, and a list of stores that sell each item. OpenAI's help center says these product results are not ads, that ads are shown separately, and that ChatGPT chooses products based on the person's query and context, including things like Memory and custom instructions (OpenAI, 2026).

OpenAI lists the main inputs. ChatGPT considers structured metadata from first-party and third-party providers, such as price and product description, along with other third-party content. When a product is sold by more than one store, merchants are ranked on factors like availability, price, quality, and whether the merchant is the maker or primary seller of the item (OpenAI, 2026). So the question isn't only whether your product is good. It's whether ChatGPT has clean data saying what you sell, what it costs, and that it's in stock.

Who this applies to

This matters most for businesses that sell physical products online: boutiques, specialty retailers, makers, and local shops with an e-commerce site. It matters less for service businesses like dentists, law firms, or contractors, whose AI visibility depends more on reviews, listings, and their website than on a product catalog. If you sell both, such as a med spa that also sells skincare, the product side is worth a look.

How we'd evaluate it

We start by asking ChatGPT the questions your customers actually ask, product by product, and recording what comes back: whether your item appears, which stores are listed for it, and what price ChatGPT shows. OpenAI notes that the price in the first response usually comes from the first merchant listed and may not be the lowest, and that price and shipping changes can take time to show up (OpenAI, 2026). A stale or wrong price is a common finding.

Then we compare that against your source data: your product pages, any feeds you send to Google Merchant Center, and what your e-commerce platform publishes. Mismatches in price, stock status, or product names between those places are the first things to fix.

Available options

  • Shopify stores. OpenAI says Shopify merchants' product data already flows into ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog, with no extra work needed (OpenAI, 2026). The job here is making that data good, not getting it connected.
  • Apply for a direct product feed. Merchants on other platforms can apply to send OpenAI a product feed. The published schema requires nine basic fields per item (ID, title, description, URL, brand, seller name, image URL, availability, and price) plus the countries you sell to and ship from (OpenAI, n.d.).
  • Strengthen your product pages. Clear titles, plain-language descriptions, real photos, visible prices, and accurate stock status help any system that reads your site. Google recommends Product structured data on product pages, and says using both structured data and a Merchant Center feed gives you the best chance of being eligible for its shopping experiences (Google Search Central, 2025b).

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The benefit is showing up at the moment someone is ready to buy, inside the answer rather than below it. The limitation is that you don't control the ranking. OpenAI's feed documentation says plainly that eligibility does not guarantee display (OpenAI, n.d.), and the help center says not every available product will be shown (OpenAI, 2026). ChatGPT may also rewrite product titles, add its own labels like "Budget-friendly," and summarize public reviews of your product, so weak reviews can show up in the answer too (OpenAI, 2026). Keeping a feed current is ongoing work, and a feed full of errors can hurt more than having none.

What we know

OpenAI has published more about how shopping results work than about most of ChatGPT's answers. The confirmed points: product results are separate from ads; selection uses structured product data from first-party and third-party sources; merchants are ranked on availability, price, quality, and maker or primary-seller status; and Shopify data is already connected (OpenAI, 2026). OpenAI's feed specification also shows that search eligibility is on by default for submitted items and that checkout is a separate integration with its own requirements (OpenAI, n.d.).

On Google's side, the guidance on AI Overviews and AI Mode is to keep doing normal SEO, keep structured data matching the visible page, and check that Merchant Center and Business Profile information is up to date. Google also says no special AI files or schema are needed to appear in those features (Google Search Central, 2025a). Put together, the overlap is clear: accurate, consistent product data in every place a machine reads it.

Next steps

Pick your five best-selling products and ask ChatGPT about each one the way a shopper would, without naming your store. Note whether you appear, what price is shown, and who else is listed. Then check that each product page shows the same price, stock status, and name that your feeds and platform report. If you're on Shopify, focus on product quality and reviews. If you're not, read OpenAI's feed documentation and decide whether applying is worth the upkeep for your catalog size.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida has a lot of small retailers competing with national chains and large marketplaces for the same product searches, often for souvenir, outdoor, and gift items that tourists shop for. Because ChatGPT gives weight to whether a merchant is the maker or primary seller of an item (OpenAI, 2026), local makers and shops with their own products may have an opening that resellers of widely available goods don't. Local pickup and shipping details belong in your product data, not just in a footer.

Frequently asked questions

Are ChatGPT shopping results ads?

OpenAI says product results in ChatGPT are not ads and that ads are shown separately. Being in a product result is not something you buy, though the inputs that feed it, like your product data, are things you can improve.

I sell on Shopify. Do I need to do anything?

According to OpenAI, Shopify merchants' product data already reaches ChatGPT through Shopify Catalog with no extra work required. That gets your data in; it doesn't make your titles, descriptions, images, or reviews any better, so those are still worth cleaning up.

Does submitting a product feed to OpenAI guarantee my products appear?

No. OpenAI's own feed documentation says eligibility does not guarantee display. A feed helps ChatGPT see accurate, current data about your products; what gets shown still depends on the shopper's question and how your offer compares.

Is this the same work as showing up in Google's AI shopping results?

Largely overlapping, but not identical. Google relies on Product structured data on your pages and on Merchant Center feeds, while ChatGPT uses first-party and third-party product metadata and its own merchant feed program. Accurate product pages help with both.
