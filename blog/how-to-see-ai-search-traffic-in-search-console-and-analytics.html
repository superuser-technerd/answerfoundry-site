How do I see AI search traffic in Google Search Console and Google Analytics?

Use two reports, because no single report shows it all. Google Search Console now has a Generative AI performance report that shows how often your pages appeared in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and it has been available to all websites worldwide since August 31, 2026. For visitors who click through from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, or Copilot, set up an “AI assistants” channel in Google Analytics 4. Neither report tells you whether AI recommends your business when a customer asks. They measure appearances and clicks, not recommendations.

What this means

Until this summer, a business owner had no direct way to see AI Overviews in Google’s own tools. AI Overviews and AI Mode data was folded into the regular “Web” numbers in Search Console. The new report pulls it out. According to Google, it shows impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode, with breakdowns by page, country, date, and device (Google Search Console Help, n.d.). An impression means a link to your site was shown to someone inside an AI feature.

Traffic from standalone AI assistants works differently. When someone clicks a link in ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude, the visit usually lands in Google Analytics 4 under “Referral,” mixed in with every other site that links to you. OpenAI says ChatGPT search automatically adds utm_source=chatgpt.com to the links it sends, so those visits can be identified in tools like Google Analytics (OpenAI, n.d.). Google’s own Analytics documentation now includes an example for grouping AI assistant traffic into its own channel (Google Analytics Help, n.d.).

Who this applies to

Any business with a website and a Google Search Console or Google Analytics 4 account. If you don’t have either, set up Search Console first. It’s free and it’s where Google reports on AI Overviews. If a web designer or agency set these up for you, ask them for access. You need Editor access or higher in Google Analytics to create channel groups (Google Analytics Help, n.d.).

How we’d evaluate it

  • Open the Generative AI performance report in Search Console. It sits with the other Performance reports. Look at which pages appear in AI features and whether that number is going up or down.
  • Compare it to your regular Performance report. Google says the AI report’s data comes from the same Web search type, so AI impressions are a slice of your overall search picture, not an addition to it (Google Search Console Help, n.d.).
  • Check Google Analytics for AI referrers. In the Traffic acquisition report, switch the first column to “Session source / medium” and search for “chat” or “perplexity” (Crestodina, 2024).
  • Look at what those visitors did. A handful of AI-referred visits that turned into calls or form fills tells you more than a large number that left right away.

Available options

  • Search Console only. Free, no setup, and covers Google’s AI features. It shows impressions, not clicks. As of Search Engine Journal’s August 31 report, the AI report did not include click data (Southern, 2026).
  • A custom channel group in Google Analytics 4. Go to Admin, then Data display, then Channel groups. Create a new group, add a channel called “AI assistants” with the condition “Source matches regex,” and drag it above “Referral” so Analytics checks it first. Google’s help page walks through this exact example and notes the grouping applies to past data as well (Google Analytics Help, n.d.). A simple pattern to start with is .*(chatgpt|openai|perplexity|claude|gemini|copilot).*. Test it against your own source list before relying on it.
  • Direct prompt testing. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google the questions your customers ask, and record who gets named. That’s the only one of the three that shows whether you’re being recommended at all.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The benefit is real, measured data instead of guesses. You can see which pages Google’s AI features show, and which AI assistants actually send people to your site.

The limitations matter just as much. Search Console’s AI report counts impressions, not clicks, and Google says sites without enough AI impressions may not see the report at all (Google Search Console Help, n.d.). Standard Google Analytics properties are limited to two custom channel groups, so if you’ve already used both, you’ll need to edit one (Google Analytics Help, n.d.). Clicks from AI Overviews and AI Mode still show up as ordinary organic search in Google Analytics, and visits from AI apps, as opposed to websites, can land in “Direct” with no source at all (Crestodina, 2024). Google’s own example pattern is also very broad. It starts by matching any source containing the letters “ai,” which can sweep in unrelated sites, so a narrower pattern is safer.

The biggest gap: none of these reports shows a customer asking ChatGPT “who’s the best [your service] near me” and getting a competitor’s name instead of yours. That conversation never touches your website, so no analytics tool can see it.

What we know

Google’s Search Console documentation states the Generative AI performance report covers AI Overviews and AI Mode, excludes Search Labs experiments, and has been rolled out to all websites worldwide as of August 31, 2026 (Google Search Console Help, n.d.). Search Engine Journal reported the same date, noted the help pages still say not every property has access yet, and reported that Google launched an opt-out control for its generative AI features alongside the reports (Southern, 2026). Google Analytics documentation provides a step-by-step “AI assistants” channel example and says to place it above Referrals (Google Analytics Help, n.d.). OpenAI documents the utm_source=chatgpt.com parameter for ChatGPT search referrals, for sites that allow its OAI-SearchBot crawler (OpenAI, n.d.).

Next steps

  1. Confirm you have access to Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4 for your site.
  2. Open the Generative AI performance report and note which pages show up.
  3. Create an “AI assistants” channel in Google Analytics and place it above Referral.
  4. Check your robots.txt to make sure you aren’t blocking OAI-SearchBot if you want ChatGPT search to link to you.
  5. Run a monthly prompt test so you know whether AI engines are naming your business, not just sending clicks.

Orlando considerations

For most Central Florida service businesses, expect small numbers at first. A local contractor or law firm might see a few AI-referred visits a month, not hundreds. That’s our expectation, not a measured figure. Small numbers still count. One visit from someone who asked ChatGPT for a recommendation and clicked through to you can be worth more than dozens of casual visits. Tracking it now gives you a baseline to compare against as more people in the Orlando area start their search with an AI assistant.

Frequently asked questions

Why don't I see the Generative AI report in Search Console?

Google says some properties may not have access yet, and sites that haven't received enough impressions in AI features may not see the report. It will also be empty if you've excluded your site from Google's generative AI features in Search Console settings.

Does the Search Console AI report show clicks?

Not as of Search Engine Journal's August 31, 2026 report. It shows impressions, meaning how often a link to your site appeared in AI Overviews or AI Mode.

Will Google Analytics catch every visit from ChatGPT?

No. Visits from ChatGPT search links usually carry utm_source=chatgpt.com, but some visits from AI apps arrive with no referrer and are counted as Direct traffic. Treat your AI numbers as a floor, not a full count.

If my AI traffic is zero, does that mean AI never mentions my business?

Not necessarily. An AI assistant can name your business without linking to your site, or the customer can call you directly without clicking. Analytics only sees visits. To know whether you're being named, you have to ask the AI engines directly.
