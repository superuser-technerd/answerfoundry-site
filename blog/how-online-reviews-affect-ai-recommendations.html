How do online reviews affect whether AI recommends your business?

Yes, they matter — but not the way a five-star average implies. AI engines don't total your stars and stop there. They scan reviews across multiple platforms, weigh how recent and specific they are, and use what they find to decide whether they trust your business enough to say your name out loud. A single glowing review barely moves that decision. Consistent, detailed reviews spread across more than one platform do.

What this means

When a customer asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI for a recommendation, the engine isn't pulling up a star rating the way a human scanning Google Maps would. It's synthesizing an answer from whatever it can find about your business across the open web — your website, directories, social mentions, and reviews wherever they're publicly visible. Reviews function as evidence in that process: they help the engine corroborate that you're a real, currently operating business that other people have actually dealt with. BrightLocal's 2026 research found that 42% of consumers now trust AI-platform recommendations as much as written reviews themselves, which means reviews and AI answers are increasingly feeding into the same trust decision rather than sitting in separate lanes (Murphy, 2026).

Who this applies to

This matters most for businesses where trust is the deciding factor before someone picks up the phone — medical and dental practices, contractors, legal services, med spas, and other categories where a bad experience is expensive to reverse. It matters less for businesses that compete almost entirely on price or availability, where reviews carry less weight in the decision itself.

How we'd evaluate this

A review audit for AI visibility looks past your star average. It checks how recently you've received reviews, whether they mention specifics (a service, a staff member, an outcome) rather than generic praise, and whether your review presence is concentrated on one platform or spread across several. It also checks whether your business name, address, and phone number are identical everywhere those reviews appear — because an AI system that finds conflicting information about who you are will discount the reviews even if they're glowing.

Available options

  • Do nothing and rely on your current review flow. Fine if you're already well-reviewed across multiple platforms and your information is consistent everywhere.
  • Run a focused review audit. Maps where your reviews actually live, how consistent your business information is, and where the gaps are versus competitors.
  • Build an ongoing review and monitoring strategy. Systematic review requests, response management, and monthly tracking of how AI engines describe you as a result.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The upside of investing in reviews is that it's one of the few AI-visibility levers a business fully controls — you can't rewrite Google's algorithm, but you can ask happy customers to leave detailed feedback. The limitation is that AI engines don't have equal access to every review platform. BrightLocal's own research team has noted that tools like ChatGPT can't see inside Google's walled garden of reviews, meaning a business with excellent Google reviews but nothing elsewhere may still be invisible to AI systems pulling from different sources (Murphy, 2026). It's also worth knowing that uniformly perfect reviews can backfire: in a separate national study, 38% of consumers said they question a business's credibility when every single review is positive (Sogolytics, 2025).

What we know about how AI weighs reviews

The research converges on a few consistent points. First, AI-driven discovery is growing fast: the share of consumers using AI to find local businesses climbed from 6% to 45% in a year, and 64% of active AI users say they trust tools like ChatGPT as much as reviews when getting a local recommendation (Murphy, 2026). Second, trust isn't blind — 88% of AI users fact-check what they're told, either by checking whether a review looks legitimate or by tracing it back to its source (Murphy, 2026). Third, reviews are only one input among many; Search Engine Journal's reporting on local AI citations found that AI systems cross-reference a business's information across every directory and platform they can find, and inconsistent details in any one of them lower the AI's confidence in the business as a whole, which lowers the odds of being cited or recommended at all (Heitzman, 2026). Fourth, absence carries its own signal — 13% of consumers in the Sogolytics study said a business missing from AI results reads as "less established" or "less trustworthy" (Sogolytics, 2025), even before a single review is factored in.

Next steps

Start by finding out where your reviews actually live today — not just Google, but Yelp, Facebook, industry-specific directories, and any platform your customers might naturally use. Check that your business name, address, and phone number match exactly across all of them. Then build a simple, consistent habit of asking satisfied customers for detailed reviews rather than a one-time push, since recency matters as much as volume.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida's density means most local categories — med spas, dental practices, home services, law firms — have several competitors within a few miles, often with overlapping names or service descriptions. That makes consistent, well-distributed reviews more of a differentiator here than in markets with less overlap, since AI engines have more competing businesses to sort through when deciding who to name.

Frequently asked questions

Does having only 5-star reviews help or hurt my AI visibility?

It can hurt. Research on consumer trust found that a meaningful share of people question credibility when every review is positive, and AI systems trained partly on human sentiment patterns tend to treat suspiciously uniform praise the same way. A realistic mix, with a few 4-star reviews and evidence you respond to concerns, reads as more credible than a perfect record.

Do I need reviews on more than just Google?

Yes, if you want AI engines to find them. Most AI assistants cannot see inside Google's review data directly, so a business with excellent Google reviews but nothing elsewhere may still be invisible to AI systems that pull from other public sources, directories, and platforms.

How many reviews are "enough" for AI to trust my business?

There's no published threshold, and it varies by category and competition. What matters more than a specific count is consistency: recent reviews, specific detail, and the same business information repeated accurately across every platform that mentions you.

Can I fix this without hiring an agency?

Some of it, yes. Claiming and completing profiles on the review platforms your customers actually use, responding to reviews, and fixing inconsistent business information are all things an owner can do directly. An audit helps most when you need to know which gaps are actually costing you visibility versus which ones don't matter much.
