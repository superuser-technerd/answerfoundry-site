How do I know if ChatGPT or Google AI recommends my business?

Run this test before you read another word: open ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google, and ask it the question your next customer is actually going to ask — not your business name, but the way a stranger who's never heard of you would phrase it. Then check whether you're in the answer. That's the whole test, and most business owners have never run it.

What this means

A Google ranking is a position in a list — ten or more results, sorted by relevance, where a customer scrolls until something looks right. An AI answer is a synthesis. The engine reads across your website, your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and anything else it can find, then decides whether it's confident enough to say your name out loud as one of a handful of options. If it can't verify who you are and what you actually do, it leaves you out — even if you outrank the businesses it did mention.

That's why a technically well-optimized site can still fail this test. Fast pages and clean structure earn Google's trust. They don't automatically earn an AI engine's trust, because the AI engine is asking a different question: not "is this page relevant," but "can I confidently vouch for this business by name."

Who this test works best for

Any business that competes on local intent — "near me," "best in [city]," "who should I call for [service]" — benefits from running this. That covers most medical and dental practices, law firms, contractors, home services companies, and other professional services with real local competition. Businesses that sell almost entirely through paid ads or an existing referral network will get less signal from it, since AI-driven discovery isn't their primary channel yet.

How we'd evaluate the results

A professional audit goes further than the DIY version. It checks whether your name, address, and phone number are identical across your website, Google Business Profile, and every directory that lists you. It reviews the recency and specificity of your reviews, not just the star average. It checks for structured data — the behind-the-scenes markup that tells search and AI systems exactly what kind of business you are. And it tests the same question across multiple engines and phrasings, not just one, to separate a real gap from a one-off fluke.

Available options

There are three ways to act on what you find:

  • Run the DIY test and stop there. Free, directional, and better than nothing — but it tells you whether there's a problem, not why.
  • Book a full AI visibility audit. Maps the specific gaps — inconsistent listings, thin content, missing structured data — and prioritizes what to fix first.
  • Ongoing monitoring. AI answers shift as engines re-crawl and re-weigh sources. Monthly tracking catches drift before a competitor quietly takes the slot you had.

Benefits, limitations, and what this test won't tell you

The benefit is speed and cost: five minutes, no tools, no signup. The limitation is that it's a snapshot, not a diagnosis. It won't tell you which specific factor is holding you back, whether the issue is fixable in weeks or months, or how you compare to competitors beyond the handful of names that happened to show up. It also can't account for personalization — results can vary session to session, which is exactly why testing on more than one engine and phrasing matters.

What we know about how AI engines choose

None of the major AI providers publish the exact formula for who gets named in an answer, and what's public changes often. What's consistent across observed behavior and each company's own public documentation is that these systems favor information they can corroborate across multiple independent sources, and they're cautious about naming a business they can't verify with confidence. That's a different bar than ranking relevance, and it rewards consistency and evidence over technical optimization alone.

Next steps

Run the test today, on two or three engines, with two or three phrasings that sound like a real customer question. Write down exactly what came back — including who else got mentioned. That five minutes tells you more about where you actually stand than another month of watching your Google rankings.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida is a dense, competitive market in categories like med spas, dental practices, law firms, and home services — often with several similarly named or similarly positioned businesses within a few miles of each other. That density makes consistent, verifiable information across your website and listings more important here than in a market with less overlap, since AI engines have more competing options to choose between when deciding who to name.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from a Google ranking check?

A ranking check tells you your position in a list of links. This test tells you whether an AI engine is confident enough to name you directly, without a list at all. A business can rank well and still fail this test, and vice versa.

Can I guarantee I'll show up in ChatGPT?

No — and you should be skeptical of anyone who guarantees placement in a system they don't control. What's achievable is fixing every signal within your control and tracking the movement over time.

How often should I re-run this test?

Monthly is a reasonable cadence for most businesses. AI engines re-crawl and re-weigh sources on their own schedule, so results can shift even if nothing about your business changed.

Does this apply to online-only businesses too?

Yes, though the test questions should drop the local phrasing — ask about your product or service category broadly rather than "near me." The underlying logic, corroborated identity and evidence over ranking position, is the same.
