Home / Blog / How long until new content shows up in AI search

Published August 22, 2026

How long does it take for a new page to show up in AI search results?

There's no single number. Google's own guidance says a new page can take anywhere from several days to several months to be crawled, indexed, and made eligible for AI Overviews or AI Mode — and even after that, citation isn't permanent. An analysis of nine months of AI Overview data found that 70% of cited pages changed citation status within two to three months. If anyone offers you a fixed timeline to "get you into" an AI answer, that's a claim worth being skeptical of.

What this means

Two different clocks are running, and most people only think about one of them. The first is indexing: has Google — or Bing, which underpins ChatGPT's search results — even found and stored the page yet? The second is citation: once a page is indexed, does an AI engine's model actually pull it into an answer, and does it keep pulling it in over time? Google is explicit that there's no separate "AI index." A page has to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet in classic Search before it can ever appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode (Google Search Central, 2025). Indexing itself isn't instant or guaranteed: Google's own documentation says crawling and reprocessing "can take anywhere from several days to several months," depending on how often its systems decide a page needs to be revisited (Google Search Central, 2025).

ChatGPT's search feature runs on a related principle with its own crawler. OAI-SearchBot is the specific bot responsible for surfacing pages in ChatGPT's search answers — a site that disallows OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt won't appear in ChatGPT search results at all, even if it allows GPTBot for model training (OpenAI Developers, n.d.). OpenAI notes that robots.txt changes take about 24 hours to propagate through its crawling systems (OpenAI Developers, n.d.) — that's a statement about how fast a permission change takes effect, not a promise about how quickly a brand-new page gets crawled and cited in the first place.

Who this applies to

Anyone publishing a new service page, location page, or blog post and hoping it shows up when a customer asks an AI assistant a buying question. It matters most in competitive local categories — law firms, contractors, med spas, dental and healthcare practices — where several similarly positioned businesses are publishing content at the same time and competing for the same handful of citation slots in an answer.

How we'd evaluate it

We check server logs for actual crawler visits. Googlebot, Bingbot, and OAI-SearchBot each leave a verifiable user-agent string, so it's possible to confirm whether a crawler has even reached a new page before worrying about whether it's being cited. We check Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool for indexing status directly, rather than guessing from search results. And we test the exact question a customer would ask, across more than one AI engine, on a repeat schedule — a single check only tells you the answer on that one day, and citation status moves.

Available options

  • Wait it out. For a well-established, frequently-crawled site, a new page can pick up organic indexing within days. For a newer or lower-authority site, it can take a month or more with no action needed beyond publishing.
  • Request indexing directly. Google's recrawl request tool and a submitted XML sitemap don't guarantee speed, but they remove "the crawler doesn't know this page exists yet" as a variable.
  • Monitor instead of guessing. Since citation status keeps shifting even after a page is indexed, ongoing tracking catches drift that a one-time check would miss.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

Understanding the two-clock model keeps a business from mistaking "not indexed yet" for "AI doesn't like my content" — two very different problems with very different fixes. The limitation is that neither clock is fully within anyone's control. Google and OpenAI both keep their exact crawl-priority logic private, and no legitimate vendor can promise a specific page will be indexed or cited by a specific date.

What we know

Google's own guidance confirms indexing timelines vary from days to months, and that meeting the technical requirements for classic Search is the only path to eligibility for AI Overviews or AI Mode — there's no separate markup or shortcut (Google Search Central, 2025). OpenAI's crawler documentation confirms OAI-SearchBot is the specific, controllable crawler behind ChatGPT search citations, and that robots.txt changes take roughly 24 hours to take effect (OpenAI Developers, n.d.). And citation, once earned, isn't permanent: Search Engine Land's analysis of nine months and more than 51,000 tracked AI Overview events found that 70% of cited pages changed citation status within two to three months (Galinos, 2026) — meaning getting indexed and cited once is a starting point, not a finish line.

Next steps

If you just published something, check your server logs or Search Console for a Googlebot or OAI-SearchBot visit before assuming it's being ignored. If it's been crawled but still isn't showing up in an AI answer for the question you'd expect it to answer, that's worth investigating on its own — inconsistent business details, thin content, or missing structured data are more common culprits than timing.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida's home-services, legal, dental, and med spa categories are dense enough that several competitors are often publishing new content in the same week. In a market like that, the businesses that show up consistently tend to be the ones that also submit sitemaps and monitor indexing status, rather than the ones that simply publish and wait.

Frequently asked questions

Does paying for ads speed up indexing?

No. Paid search and organic indexing are separate systems. Running ads doesn't get a page crawled or cited by an AI engine any faster.

Can I force Google or ChatGPT to index a page immediately?

Not fully. Google's recrawl request tool and a submitted sitemap can prompt a visit sooner, and allowing OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt is required for ChatGPT search — but neither guarantees a specific timeline.

If a page was cited once, will it stay cited?

Not necessarily. Citation status shifts as engines re-crawl and re-weigh sources, so a page that showed up in an AI answer last month can drop out even with no changes on your end.

Does blocking AI crawlers help or hurt?

It depends on the goal. Blocking OAI-SearchBot or Googlebot removes you from AI answers entirely; allowing them is a prerequisite for citation, not a guarantee of it.
