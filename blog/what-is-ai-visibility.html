What Is AI Visibility?

AI visibility is how reliably and accurately a business gets named, described, and recommended by AI systems — tools like ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Perplexity, and Microsoft Copilot — when someone asks a question that business could answer. It's a separate thing from where a website ranks in a traditional search results page, and increasingly, it's the thing that determines whether a prospective customer ever hears a business's name at all.

If that sounds like a small distinction, consider what's actually changed in how people search.

What changed: search became a conversation

For roughly two decades, "searching" meant typing a few keywords into a search box and scanning a page of blue links. The business's job was to rank high enough on that page to get clicked.

That's no longer the only path a customer takes. A growing share of people now ask a question directly to an AI system and expect a synthesized answer — not a list of links to sort through themselves. Someone looking for a dentist in Winter Park might type "best dentist in Winter Park for a nervous adult patient" into ChatGPT rather than Google. Someone comparing med spas might ask Perplexity to summarize the differences between two clinics near them. Someone researching a home-service company might get a direct recommendation inside a Google AI Overview — the AI-generated summary box that now appears above traditional search results for many queries — without ever scrolling further down the page.

In each case, the AI system is doing something search engines historically didn't do: it's picking a small number of businesses to actually name, and leaving the rest unmentioned. Industry observers have widely noted this shift toward answer-based search since Google introduced AI Overviews in 2024 and as tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity grew as everyday research tools — though the exact share of searches this affects varies by industry and is still being studied. What's consistent is the pattern: fewer clicks through a list, more trust placed directly in a generated answer.

That shift creates a new, specific question for every local and professional-service business: when someone asks an AI system about what you do, does it name you — and does it describe you accurately?

Why traditional SEO metrics don't answer this question

A business can have solid Google rankings, a healthy volume of organic traffic, and a well-optimized website, and still be functionally invisible to AI systems. That's because AI visibility depends on a different set of inputs than ranking position does.

Search engine rankings measure how well a page matches a query and how authoritative it appears, then return a ranked list for a human to evaluate. AI systems work differently. Many of them use a technique called retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — in plain terms, the AI searches for and pulls in relevant information from multiple sources, then writes a new, synthesized answer in its own words, often citing only a handful of those sources or naming only a few businesses by name. Rank position on a search page doesn't map cleanly onto whether a business gets pulled into that synthesis. A business ranking #1 for a keyword can still be left out of the AI's answer if the underlying information about it is inconsistent, thin, or harder for the system to summarize confidently than a competitor's.

That's the gap AI visibility measures: not "do I rank," but "does the AI trust what it can find about me enough to say my name."

A plain-language framework for thinking about AI visibility

At AnswerFoundry, we assess AI visibility across six categories. Together, they roughly correspond to the structure behind our internal 100-point scoring framework, though we don't publish the exact point weighting — the categories themselves are useful on their own, even without a formal score attached.

1. Entity accuracy. An "entity" is simply the AI's internal concept of your business — your name, address, phone number, services, hours, and the relationships between them (which location does what, which provider treats which condition, and so on). If your business name is listed three different ways across your website, Google Business Profile, and directory listings, or your hours are wrong on one platform, that inconsistency makes it harder for an AI system to be confident about who you are — and AI systems tend to avoid naming a business with information they can't confirm.

2. Technical readiness. This covers whether your website is structured in a way machines can actually parse — including schema markup, a standardized code added to a webpage that explicitly labels things like your business type, services, and reviews for machines to read, rather than leaving them for a human to infer from paragraph text.

3. Content coverage. Does your website actually contain direct, clear answers to the specific questions your customers ask? A page that describes services in vague marketing language is harder for an AI to summarize confidently than a page that plainly states what a service involves, who it's for, and what it costs or how pricing works.

4. Authority and citations. A citation, in this context, is any time a credible outside source — a review platform, industry directory, news article, or professional association — mentions your business. AI systems tend to trust businesses that show up consistently across multiple credible sources rather than businesses that only talk about themselves on their own website.

5. Competitive share of answer. This measures how often your business gets named relative to your direct competitors when the same category of question is asked. Two businesses can both have "good" visibility in isolation, but one may still be named 80% of the time while the other is named 20% of the time.

6. Monitoring. AI-generated answers are not static — they change as the underlying information about your business, your competitors, and the web changes. Treating AI visibility as a one-time project rather than an ongoing thing to monitor is one of the most common mistakes businesses make.

Where to start

You don't need a formal audit to get a first read on where you stand. Start by asking ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google directly the kinds of questions your customers actually ask — "best [your service] near [your city]," or a specific question about a service you offer — and see whether you're named, and whether what's said about you is accurate.

AI visibility isn't about controlling what an AI says — no one can guarantee that. It's about giving AI systems accurate, consistent, well-structured information to work with, so that when they do generate an answer, your business has a fair chance of being part of it.
