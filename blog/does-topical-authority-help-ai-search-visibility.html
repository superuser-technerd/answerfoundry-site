Does topical authority help AI search visibility?

Probably, a little. Topical authority means covering one subject thoroughly enough that search engines and AI engines treat your site as a reliable source on it. The best available data suggests AI engines will cite almost any site as a source, but they name and recommend businesses mostly in the subjects those businesses are known for. The measured effect is small, it varies by industry, and no amount of content guarantees a mention.

What this means

It’s easy to assume topical authority is a score Google keeps for every website. It isn’t, at least not one Google has published. The term comes from the SEO industry. Google has confirmed one related system: “topic authority,” which it uses to decide which publications are expert sources for news queries in areas such as health, politics, or finance (Granito, 2023). That post is about news publishers. It doesn’t describe a score for a plumber’s or an accountant’s website.

The idea carries over to AI search for a practical reason. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use a “query fan-out” technique, “issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources,” to build a response (Google Search Central, 2025). One customer question becomes several searches. A site that covers the surrounding subtopics has more pages that can match one of them.

Who this applies to

It applies to any business that publishes content to be found: law firms, contractors, accounting and financial practices, real estate teams, clinics. It matters most for businesses deciding what to write next, and for those tempted to publish on subjects far from what they sell because a keyword tool showed search volume there. It matters less for a business with almost no content at all. That business has a more basic problem than focus.

How we’d evaluate it

We’d start with the questions, not the pages. List the 20 or so questions a customer asks before hiring someone in your field. Mark which ones your site answers on a page of its own, in your own words. Then ask those questions in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, and Perplexity several times each, and record two separate things: whether your site is cited as a source, and whether your business is named in the answer. Those are different outcomes, and the research below treats them differently. A subject where you’re cited but never named is one where the AI trusts your page and still doesn’t recommend you.

Available options

  • Go deeper on what you already do. Cover your core services with pages that answer the real follow-up questions: cost, timing, risks, who it isn’t for.
  • Connect the pages. Google lists “making your content easily findable through internal links on your website” among the practices that still apply to AI features (Google Search Central, 2025).
  • Write from experience. Google’s guidance favors “non-commodity” content that offers a first-hand view over summaries of what’s already published (Google Search Central, 2026).
  • Leave distant subjects alone. A roofing company’s article on mortgage rates may get cited. It’s unlikely to get the roofer recommended.
  • Don’t build a page for every phrasing. Google says creating separate content for every search variation, done primarily to manipulate rankings or AI responses, violates its scaled content abuse policy (Google Search Central, 2026).

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

Depth on your core subject helps ordinary search and gives customers better answers, so the work has value even if AI engines change how they choose sources. The limitation is size. The one large study we found describes its own effects as “very light” and says other factors, such as overall brand authority and third-party mentions, may outweigh topical authority (Indig, 2026). The tradeoff is time. Every in-depth page on your main service is a page you didn’t write on something else, and there is no published number of pages at which a site becomes an authority.

What we know

The most direct evidence is a study by Kevin Indig using Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit data: US ChatGPT answers across 1,094 categories, five prompt variants per category, January through June 2026 (Indig, 2026). It separated citations, where a site is listed as a source, from mentions, where a brand is named in the answer.

In categories close to a brand’s established expertise, 74% of its appearances were citations, 44% were named mentions, and 34% were both. In categories distant from that expertise, 50% were citations, 25% were mentions, and 9% were both. Indig’s summary: a site can be a source on almost any topic, but the AI recommends it in its core topics.

Breadth without depth looked like a liability for mentions. A brand named in only one of five prompts across its categories was associated with a lower share of mentions the following month (-0.051), while brands appearing in all five were slightly positive. The pattern differed by industry. Finance and real estate showed the strongest link between repeated coverage and later citations. In legal and healthcare, the association with named mentions stayed negative even at full coverage (-0.058 and -0.037).

The limits are real. The study covers one AI engine and six months. Every figure is an association after statistical controls, and the author states the models can’t prove that publishing caused the later result. The data came from Semrush, which sells AI visibility software and owns Search Engine Land, where the study was republished. We haven’t found an independent replication, and the study didn’t isolate local service businesses.

Google’s own guidance points the same direction without numbers. It tells site owners that “a high quantity of pages doesn’t make a website higher quality or more relevant to users” (Google Search Central, 2026). Its advice to news publishers on topic authority was to “provide great coverage about the areas and topics they know well” (Granito, 2023).

Next steps

Write down the three services that bring in most of your revenue. For each one, list the questions customers ask before they hire you and check whether your site answers each on its own page. Fill those gaps before writing about anything else. Then run the questions through two or three AI engines and note where you’re cited, where you’re named, and where you’re absent. Repeat in 60 to 90 days. If nothing has changed, the constraint may be outside your website: reviews, directory listings, or mentions on other sites.

Orlando considerations

For a Central Florida business, the subject is usually a service in a place. A Winter Park estate planning attorney is better served by thorough pages on Florida probate, homestead rules, and guardianship than by general articles on personal finance. A Clermont roofer has more to say about tile roofs, hurricane straps, and insurance inspections than a national site does. That local, first-hand detail is the kind of coverage a larger competitor can’t easily copy. Whether an AI engine rewards it for a given question is something you have to test.

Frequently asked questions

What is topical authority?

Topical authority is an SEO term for how thoroughly and credibly a website covers one subject. A site that answers the main questions in its field, in its own words and from its own experience, is said to have more of it than a site with one thin page on the same subject. Google has confirmed a system called topic authority for news queries. It hasn’t published a general topical authority score for all websites.

How many pages does a business need to build topical authority?

There’s no number. Google says a high quantity of pages doesn’t make a website higher quality or more relevant, and that creating separate pages for every search variation mainly to manipulate results violates its spam policy. A handful of pages that answer the real questions customers ask about your core services is a sounder goal than a page count.

Does topical authority matter more for some industries than others?

One 2026 study of ChatGPT answers suggests it does. Finance and real estate showed the strongest link between repeated coverage and later citations. In legal and healthcare, broader coverage was associated with a lower share of named brand mentions even at full coverage. The study measured associations in one AI engine over six months, so it shows a pattern and not a cause.

Can AnswerFoundry guarantee that building topical authority will get my business named by AI?

No. AI engines are third-party systems that nobody outside those companies controls, and the measured effect of topical authority is small. What we can do is test which questions in your field AI engines currently answer with your business, find the subjects where your coverage is thin, and track whether the answers change.
