Published November 21, 2026

How do I get my business to show up in Google Gemini answers?

You can't sign up for it, pay for it, or submit your business to it. The Gemini app can pull fresh information from Google Search when it answers, so the things that get you into Gemini are mostly the things that get you properly into Google: a crawlable, indexed website with clear facts, and consistent information about your business elsewhere on the web. There's also one robots.txt setting that can quietly keep your site out of Gemini's answers, and it's worth checking today.

What this means

Gemini is Google's AI assistant, separate from the AI Overviews you see at the top of a Google search. It's big. On Alphabet's July 2026 earnings call, CEO Sundar Pichai said the Gemini app had 950 million monthly active users (Pichai, 2026). Some of those people ask it the same thing they used to type into Google: who should I hire, which practice is good, who's open near me.

A Gemini answer can come from two places. One is what the model learned in training. The other is grounding, which means pulling in content from Google's Search index while it writes the answer. Google's crawler documentation describes grounding in Gemini Apps as "providing content from the Google Search index to the model at prompt time to improve factuality and relevancy" (Google, 2026). For a local business, grounding matters most, because training data goes stale and grounding doesn't.

Who this applies to

This matters most for businesses whose customers do research before they buy: law firms, contractors and home services, accounting and financial practices, real estate, healthcare and aesthetics, and other professional services. It matters less for businesses that get almost all their customers through referrals or paid ads. Even then, a prospect may still ask Gemini about you before they call, so it's worth knowing what it says.

How we'd evaluate it

We'd start by asking Gemini the questions a real customer would ask, including "best [service] in [city]" and your business name, and logging the date, the exact prompt, and what came back. Then we'd open the Sources panel, if there is one, to see which pages Gemini drew from. Next we'd check the technical basics: is the site indexed in Google, can Googlebot crawl it, and does robots.txt block the Google-Extended token? Last, we'd compare what Gemini says against your website, Google Business Profile, and major directories, to see where wrong details are coming from.

Available options

  • Check robots.txt for Google-Extended. If your site blocks it, Google says your content won't be used to ground answers in Gemini Apps (Google, 2026). Some site owners block it on purpose to keep content out of AI training. That's a fair choice, but make it knowingly, not because a plugin or template did it for you. Our post on whether to block AI crawlers covers the tradeoff.
  • Get the Google Search basics right. For AI Overviews and AI Mode, Google says a page only has to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, with "no additional technical requirements" (Google Search Central, 2025). Google hasn't published a separate eligibility checklist for the Gemini app. But since Gemini grounds in the same Search index, a page that isn't indexed can't be grounded on.
  • Make your facts easy to pull. Say plainly on your site what you do, where, for whom, and how to reach you. A sentence a model can lift cleanly is more useful than a clever tagline.
  • Keep your Google listings consistent. Some Gemini features, like Ask Maps, use Google Maps data directly. See how Maps data feeds Gemini.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The upside is that none of this is Gemini-only work. Clear, crawlable, consistent information also helps you in regular Google results, AI Overviews, and other AI assistants. The limits are real, though. Google says not every Gemini response includes sources (Google, n.d.-b), so you can be described without getting a link. Google also warns that Gemini "can hallucinate and present inaccurate information as factual" (Google, n.d.-a). Even if you do everything right, Gemini may still leave you out or get a detail wrong, and you can't directly edit what it says.

What we know

  • Gemini has a large audience. Alphabet reported 950 million monthly active users for the Gemini app on July 22, 2026, with daily active users tripling over the previous year (Pichai, 2026).
  • Gemini can ground answers in Google Search. Google's developer documentation says grounding with Google Search connects Gemini to real-time web content, so it can cite verifiable sources beyond its knowledge cutoff (Google AI for Developers, n.d.). That page covers the developer API, but the crawler documentation confirms that Gemini Apps also use Search-index grounding (Google, 2026).
  • One robots.txt token controls it. Google-Extended controls whether your content can be used to train future Gemini models and to ground answers in Gemini Apps. Google says it doesn't affect inclusion in Google Search and isn't a ranking signal (Google, 2026).
  • Sources are shown sometimes, not always. When sources are available, they appear in a Sources panel at the bottom of the response or inline. If Gemini quotes a large amount of text from a webpage, it links that page (Google, n.d.-b).
  • Nothing is guaranteed. For Google's AI search features, Google says meeting every requirement still "doesn't mean that Google will crawl, index, or serve" your content (Google Search Central, 2025).

Next steps

Open your site's robots.txt (yourdomain.com/robots.txt) and search it for "Google-Extended." Then ask Gemini three questions a customer would ask about your type of business in your city, plus one question about your business by name. Write down what it says and which sources it shows. If the details are wrong, our guide to fixing incorrect AI answers is the next read.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida has a lot of same-category competitors packed close together, and many have similar names. When Gemini looks up "best [service] in Orlando," it has plenty of options and limited room. Inconsistent addresses, old suite numbers, or a Kissimmee listing that conflicts with a Winter Park listing make it harder for Gemini to be sure which business is which. Businesses near the tourist corridors should also keep in mind that visitors may ask Gemini about them from out of state, before they ever arrive.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Gemini app the same thing as Google AI Overviews?

No. AI Overviews and AI Mode are features inside Google Search. The Gemini app is a separate assistant at gemini.google.com and in Google's mobile apps. Both can draw on Google Search, but they are different products, and Google documents and controls them separately.

Will blocking Google-Extended hurt my Google rankings?

According to Google's crawler documentation, Google-Extended does not affect a site's inclusion in Google Search and is not used as a ranking signal. It controls whether your content can be used to train future Gemini models and to ground answers in Gemini Apps.

Why doesn't Gemini show sources for every answer?

Google's own help page says not all Gemini responses include sources or related links. If there's no Sources button under a response, Gemini didn't provide links for that answer. A business can be described without being linked.

Can AnswerFoundry guarantee my business shows up in Gemini?

No. Gemini is a Google product outside our control, and Google itself says meeting every requirement doesn't guarantee your content will be crawled, indexed, or served. What we can do is test what Gemini says about you and fix the signals within your control.
