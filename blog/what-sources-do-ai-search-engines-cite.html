What sources do AI search engines cite when recommending a business?

Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI where to find a service, then look at every source name or link the answer actually drops in. Three separate 2025-2026 studies, together covering well over a hundred million individual citations, agree on one thing: a small, shifting set of third-party platforms — not your own website — account for most of what these engines point to. Knowing which platforms matter for your category is a more useful question than "am I optimized," and it's a different question than the one most local SEO advice answers.

What this means

When an AI engine names a business, it's often drawing on two separate things: what it already "knows" from training data, and what it retrieves live from the web to back that answer up. Orbit Media's tracking of 13,184 citations across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity found that citations are close to a strict subset of mentions — a brand gets named far more often than it gets linked (Orbit Media Studios, 2026). For a well-known brand, that gap can be enormous: in that same dataset, Semrush (as the tracked client) was mentioned in 83-100% of relevant answers across every model, but its citation rate on those same answers swung from 83% down to 0% week to week.

The practical read: showing up in an AI answer isn't only about your own site ranking well. It's also about whether the third-party sources these engines already trust — review platforms, industry directories, comparison sites, editorial coverage — mention you accurately and consistently.

Who this applies to

This matters most for businesses where a buyer is likely to ask an AI engine a comparison or recommendation question — "best [service] near me," "who should I hire for [service]," "is [category] worth it." That covers most local and professional services: law firms, home services and contractors, financial and accounting practices, real estate, and healthcare and aesthetics. It matters less for businesses that win almost entirely through existing referral networks or paid channels, where AI-driven discovery isn't yet a meaningful part of how customers find them.

How we'd evaluate it

A proper audit doesn't stop at checking your own site. It maps which third-party sources are already being cited for your specific category and market — which review platforms, which industry directories, which local or trade publications — and then checks whether your business shows up on those sources accurately, with consistent name, address, phone, and service details. It also runs the same test across more than one engine, since the research below shows engines don't agree with each other or, week to week, with themselves.

Available options

  • Run the DIY version. Ask two or three AI engines your buyer's real question and note every source name or link they cite. Free, directional, but a single snapshot.
  • Full AI visibility audit. Identifies the specific third-party sources carrying weight in your category and flags where your listing is missing, thin, or inconsistent.
  • Ongoing monitoring. Citation sources move — sometimes sharply, as the Reddit example below shows. Monthly tracking catches a shift before it costs you the slot.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The benefit of understanding citation sources is that it points effort somewhere concrete: specific platforms, not a vague "be more visible to AI" goal. The limitation is that citation patterns are genuinely volatile and engine-specific, so a source that mattered last quarter may not this quarter, and what wins on one engine may do nothing on another. None of the research below should be read as a fixed list to chase forever — it's a snapshot of a moving target, useful for direction, not a permanent playbook.

What we know about citation sources

Three independent analyses, run at different scales and time windows, converge on the same broad pattern while disagreeing on the details — which is itself informative.

Search Engine Land's coverage of Peec AI's analysis of 30 million cited sources across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews found Reddit ranked as the single most-cited domain overall, followed by YouTube and LinkedIn, with Wikipedia and Forbes also in the top five and review platforms like Yelp and G2 appearing often specifically in recommendation-style queries. The same coverage noted real engine-level differences: ChatGPT favored Wikipedia, Reddit, and editorial sites like Forbes; Google leaned toward platforms like Facebook and Yelp; Perplexity emphasized Reddit, LinkedIn, and G2 for B2B queries (Search Engine Land, 2026).

Semrush's own three-month study, tracking more than 230,000 prompts and over 100 million citations across ChatGPT search, Google AI Mode, and Perplexity between July and October 2025, documented just how fast that mix can move: ChatGPT cited Reddit in close to 60% of prompt responses in early August 2025, then around 10% by mid-September 2025, alongside a similar drop for Wikipedia. In the same window, Forbes, PRNewswire, and Medium gained ChatGPT citation share. Semrush's Head of Organic and AI Visibility, Sergei Rogulin, attributed the shift less to any single technical cause and more to the platforms deliberately reducing concentration on a handful of heavily-cited sources (Semrush, 2025).

Orbit Media Studios' independent, smaller-scale tracker (13,184 citations across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity, data through August 23, 2026) found the four engines rarely agree with each other at all: across nearly 1,800 matched query-and-domain combinations, all four models cited the same domain for the same question only 30 times — about 1.7%. Perplexity cited the most sources per answer (19.2 on average, favoring LinkedIn, YouTube, and Reddit); Claude cited the fewest (3.6 on average, favoring review sites and listicles, and never once citing Reddit in the tracked dataset) (Orbit Media Studios, 2026).

Next steps

Start by identifying, for your specific category and city, which third-party sources keep showing up when you or a customer asks an AI engine your buyer's real question — not a generic industry list, your actual category. Then check your listing on each one for accuracy and consistency. Re-run the check on more than one engine, since the research above shows they don't agree with each other, and revisit it periodically rather than treating any single snapshot as settled.

Orlando considerations

For Central Florida service businesses, the sources worth checking usually split into two buckets: general platforms that show up across categories (Google Business Profile, Yelp, better-known review sites) and category-specific ones (Avvo or Martindale for law firms, Houzz or Angi for home services, Healthgrades or Zocdoc for healthcare, Zillow for real estate). In a dense market like Orlando, where several similarly positioned businesses often compete within a few miles of each other, being the accurate, consistently-listed option on the sources an AI engine already trusts matters more than it would in a market with less overlap.

Frequently asked questions

Does this mean I should focus on getting cited on Reddit?

Not necessarily. Reddit's share of citations has swung sharply by engine and by month — Semrush's research recorded ChatGPT citing Reddit in close to 60% of prompt responses in early August 2025, then around 10% by mid-September 2025. Chasing whichever platform is trending this month is less durable than showing up consistently on the third-party sources that already cover your specific category.

If AI engines rarely cite my own website, is my website still worth investing in?

Yes. Orbit Media's tracking found that citations are almost a strict subset of mentions — engines routinely name a brand without linking to it, especially once that brand is well-established in the model's training data. A strong website still feeds what the model already knows about you; it just isn't the only, or even the main, source an engine points to.

Do all AI engines cite the same sources?

No. Orbit Media's tracker found that across nearly 1,800 matched query-and-domain combinations, all four models it tracked (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity) agreed on the same cited domain only 30 times — about 1.7%. Search Engine Land's coverage of Peec AI's 30-million-source analysis similarly found each engine leans on a different mix: ChatGPT toward Wikipedia and editorial sites, Google toward platforms like Facebook and Yelp, Perplexity toward Reddit, LinkedIn, and G2.

Can AnswerFoundry guarantee our business gets cited on a specific platform?

No — and you should be skeptical of anyone who guarantees a specific citation or placement inside a system they don't control. What's achievable is identifying which third-party sources already carry weight in your category and making sure your business is accurately and consistently represented on them.
