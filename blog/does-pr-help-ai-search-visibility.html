Does PR help with AI search visibility?

Yes — earned media appears to be one of the more useful levers a business has, because AI answer engines lean heavily on what independent third parties say about you rather than on what you say about yourself. But PR is a slow, indirect lever with no guaranteed outcome, and a press release blasted to a syndication wire is not the same thing as coverage.

What this means

SEO earns you a slot in a list of links. AEO — answer engine optimization — earns you a spot inside an AI-generated answer, with no list at all. That difference changes what counts as evidence. A search engine can rank a page it has never seen corroborated anywhere else. An AI engine writing a sentence that names your business is making a claim on your behalf, and it tends to make that claim about businesses it has seen described consistently, by more than one source, in more than one place.

PR produces exactly that kind of material: a description of your business written by someone who isn't you, published somewhere you don't own. That's the whole mechanism. It isn't magic and it isn't a ranking factor anyone has published — it's corroboration, and corroboration is what these systems appear to reward.

Who this applies to

Any business in a category where a customer makes a considered, high-stakes hiring decision — law firms, contractors and home services, accounting and financial practices, real estate, healthcare and aesthetics, and similar professional services. These are the categories where an AI engine has to pick a small handful of names out of many plausible options, and where third-party corroboration does the most work.

It applies less if your customers arrive almost entirely through paid ads or an established referral network. PR aimed at AI visibility is a long-horizon investment, not a fix for this quarter.

How we'd evaluate it

Before recommending PR to anyone, we'd want to know three things. First, whether an AI engine can already describe your business accurately. Second, where outside sources describe you at all — directories, review platforms, association listings, local news, trade press. Third, whether those descriptions consistently connect you to the same service category, or scatter across unrelated ones.

That third point is the one most businesses miss. Ten mentions that all describe you the same way are worth more than thirty that each describe you differently.

Available options

  • Fix your own signals first. Consistent name, address, and phone across your site and every listing; structured data that matches your visible content; an About page that states plainly what you do. This is cheaper than PR and has to happen anyway.
  • Pursue genuine editorial coverage. Trade publications, local business press, industry podcasts, expert commentary. Slower and less predictable, but it produces the independent description that PR is actually for.
  • Skip wire-service press releases as an AI visibility play. Syndicated releases mostly generate near-duplicate copies of your own text across low-authority sites. That's repetition, not corroboration.
  • Track mentions, not just links. If you're going to invest in PR, measure unlinked brand mentions alongside backlinks, since the two behave differently in AI answers.

Benefits, limitations, and tradeoffs

The benefit is durability. A mention in a respected trade publication doesn't expire the way an ad does, and it works on human readers and AI systems at the same time. The limitations are real, though. PR is expensive relative to fixing your own site, its results are unpredictable — you can pitch ten outlets and place zero — and nobody, including us, can guarantee that a specific piece of coverage produces a specific AI recommendation.

There's also a sequencing trap. Coverage amplifies whatever an AI engine already believes about you. If your entity is inconsistent — three different phone numbers across listings, a service description that doesn't match your site — earned media adds volume to a muddled signal. Fix the foundation first.

What we know

Google's own documentation is explicit that there are no special optimizations for AI Overviews or AI Mode: "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary," and no new machine-readable files or markup are needed (Google Search Central, n.d.). What Google does emphasize, in its guidance on helpful content, is reputation as assessed from the outside — one of its self-assessment questions asks whether someone researching the site producing the content "would come away with an impression that it is well-trusted or widely-recognized as an authority on its topic" (Google Search Central, 2025). That is an off-site signal by definition, and PR is one of the few things that moves it.

On the measurement side, Semrush's 2026 study with Kevin Indig logged 3,981 domain appearances across 115 prompts, 14 countries, and four AI engines. It found that about 62% of AI citations were "ghost citations" — the engine used a page as a source but never named the brand in the answer — and that citation and mention behavior diverge sharply by engine: Gemini named brands in 83.7% of appearances while linking only 21.4% of the time, and ChatGPT did close to the reverse, citing 87% of the time and mentioning brands in 20.7% of answers (Loktionova, 2026). The study's own recommendation for consumer-facing brands was to track unlinked mentions on third-party sites — news coverage, community threads, listicles — alongside backlinks, on the reasoning that both feed an engine's familiarity with a brand.

Industry coverage has been making the same argument from the practitioner side, arguing that AI search elevates reputation and entity recognition over traditional ranking tactics, and that mentions in trade publications and local outlets carry more weight than a brand's own blog (Jacobson, 2025). We'd treat that as a reasonable read of observed behavior rather than a measured law — no AI provider publishes the weighting, and it changes.

Next steps

Before spending anything on PR, run the cheap test. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google the question a stranger looking for your service would type. Note who gets named. Then search your own business name and read what the AI says about you — not whether it's flattering, but whether it's accurate. If the description is wrong or vague, that's a signals problem, and it's fixable without a publicist. If the description is accurate but you're simply never in the recommendation set, that's when earned media starts to earn its cost.

Orlando considerations

Central Florida has a dense field of similarly positioned businesses in categories like law, home services, aesthetics, and real estate — often several within a few miles carrying near-identical service descriptions. An AI engine there has more competing candidates and less to distinguish them by. Local business press, chamber and association coverage, and regional trade outlets are usually more accessible for an Orlando business than national placements, and they're better matched to the local intent behind the question anyway.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a press release to show up in AI answers?

No. A press release distributed to a syndication wire mostly produces near-identical copies of the same text across low-authority sites. What appears to matter more is genuine editorial coverage — a reporter or publication independently describing your business — because that is harder to manufacture and reads as corroboration rather than repetition.

Does a media mention have to include a link to count?

Not necessarily. Semrush's 2026 ghost citations study found that citations and unlinked brand mentions behave as separate outcomes across AI engines, and that some engines name brands in an answer far more often than they link to them. Tracking unlinked mentions alongside backlinks gives you a fuller picture than backlinks alone.

How long does earned coverage take to affect AI answers?

There is no published timeline from any AI provider, and we don't have a dated measurement of our own to cite. AI engines re-crawl, re-index, and retrain on their own schedules, and some answers draw on live retrieval while others draw on training data. Treat any specific promised timeframe with skepticism.

Is a small trade or local publication worth pursuing?

Often, yes. A niche trade journal or local outlet that consistently associates your business with a specific service category can be more useful than a broad mention in a large publication that never connects you to what you actually do. Relevance and consistency of context matter, not just the size of the outlet.
