The Problem

Clinical reputation doesn't automatically transfer to AI

Independent practices spend years building clinical reputation, referral relationships, and patient trust — and almost none of that effort is visible to the AI systems patients now consult before they ever call your office. A patient researching a new provider, a second opinion, or a specialty treatment increasingly starts with an AI assistant instead of a search engine, and that assistant answers from whatever it can find: directory listings, review sites, your website, and third-party health content. If that information is outdated, thin, or inconsistent, the AI has little choice but to recommend the practice down the street that looks clearer and more current.

This is a different problem than traditional SEO. Ranking on page one of Google doesn't automatically mean an AI system will cite your practice, describe your services accurately, or mention you at all when a patient asks a direct question. Many practices we've evaluated have a strong web presence and still show up inconsistently — or not at all — across the platforms patients are starting to rely on.

For healthcare specifically, the stakes are higher than a missed lead. If an AI system describes your practice's services, credentials, or "accepting new patients" status incorrectly, that's not just a marketing gap — it's a patient-trust and accuracy problem.

Common Visibility Failures

Patterns we consistently see

When evaluating independent healthcare practices, the same handful of issues show up again and again.

📇

Outdated directory and NPI information

Old addresses, disconnected phone numbers, or incorrect hours still circulating across health directories and citation sources.

🔀

Inconsistent practice details

Your website says one thing, Healthgrades says another, and your Google Business Profile says a third.

🕓

Stale "accepting new patients" status

AI systems repeating outdated availability information because no one corrected it after your panel status changed.

📄

Thin or generic service descriptions

An "About Us" page that doesn't clearly state specialties, credentials, or what makes your care model different.

📊

Denser competitor citation coverage

A competitor that isn't necessarily better, but shows up more often because its information is more complete and consistent across the sources AI systems draw from.

Accuracy & Compliance

AI-generated answers don't come with disclaimers

Healthcare is a regulated industry, and AI-generated answers don't come with the same disclaimers a human referral would. If an AI system repeats an inaccurate claim about a treatment, a credential, or an insurance relationship, the burden of correcting that impression falls on your practice — often after a patient has already formed an expectation. Getting your source information accurate, current, and consistent isn't just good marketing practice; it reduces the chance that AI systems propagate something your practice would never claim on its own.

FAQ

Fair questions, straight answers

Can you guarantee my practice will be recommended by ChatGPT or other AI systems?

No. No ethical vendor can guarantee placement, citation, or recommendation in any AI system. What we can do is measure your current visibility, diagnose the gaps, and improve the signals that influence whether AI systems can find, understand, and accurately cite your practice.

How is this different from SEO?

Traditional SEO optimizes for search engine rankings. AEO (answer-engine optimization) focuses on whether AI assistants can find accurate, structured information about your practice and choose to reference it in a direct answer. The two overlap but aren't the same discipline.

Which AI platforms do you test?

We test across major consumer AI platforms patients actually use, including ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity, and update our testing set as usage shifts.

How long before I see a difference?

AI systems don't update on a fixed schedule, and re-indexing varies by platform. Visibility improvements typically build over one to two quarters as AI engines re-crawl and re-weigh sources — local categories with weak competition often move faster.
