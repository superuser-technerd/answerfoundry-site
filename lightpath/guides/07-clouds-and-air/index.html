Clouds and air — reading the forecast for light, not for rain

Weather apps are built to answer "will I get wet?" Photographers need a different question: "what will the light do?" The two things that matter most are cloud cover and how clean the air is, and Lightpath pulls both into the Shot Score. This guide explains how to read them.

Cloud cover: some is good, most is bad

The Clouds bar comes from Apple Weather's hourly cloud-cover forecast. The score treats cover up to about 30% as neutral and penalises it above that. This matches what sunsets actually do:

  • 0–20%. Clean, saturated colour on the horizon but a plain sky. Good for silhouettes and for astrophotography.
  • 20–60% with high or mid-level cloud. The sunset jackpot. Cirrus and altocumulus catch light from below after the sun drops under the horizon and turn orange, pink and red across the whole sky. The best colour often comes 10–20 minutes after sunset.
  • Low cloud or overcast. The light is blocked before it reaches anything. Flat, grey, over quickly.

The forecast gives a percentage, not a cloud type, so use the number as a prompt: at 40% cover, check a satellite or radar loop to see whether the cloud is high and thin or low and solid, and whether the western horizon is clear at sunset (the light has to get under the cloud deck).

Lightpath's Best next 24 h readout already folds cloud cover into the search, so if two golden hours are available it will point you at the clearer one.

Air: haze, smoke and AQI

The Air bar uses Open-Meteo's US AQI, a 0–500 scale where 0–50 is Good. Particulates scatter blue light and mute contrast, so a high AQI means:

  • Distant ridges lose separation and go grey.
  • Colour on the horizon shifts toward orange-brown rather than clean red and pink.
  • Star fields lose the faintest stars first; the Milky Way looks flat.

A moderate amount of haze is not always bad. Layered mountain scenes rely on aerial perspective — each ridge lighter than the one in front — and a little haze exaggerates it. Wildfire smoke, on the other hand, produces a red disc and nothing else. The number tells you which you have.

Reading the caption

Under the four bars, Lightpath states where the numbers came from. clouds via Apple Weather · air via Open-Meteo means live data. cloud and air are sample values until the forecast loads means the forecast has not arrived — you are offline, or the request failed — and only the Light bar is real. Do not plan a trip on sample values.

Forecasts are cached for 30 minutes per location per day, so the app does not hammer the network while you scrub the timeline.

Field tips

  • Cloud forecasts beyond 48 hours are guesswork. Check again the morning of.
  • The Shot Score cannot see fog. Valley fog at dawn is one of the best conditions in landscape photography and one of the worst for the forecast to predict; look at the dew point and overnight low yourself.
  • After a frontal passage the air is usually at its clearest. That is when distant peaks and the Milky Way both look best.
