How the Founder's Own Business Diagnosed Its AI Visibility Gap

PrimeCell Regenerative & Aesthetics Medicine is Kenny Chantasi's own business — the case that led him to build AnswerFoundry. It happens to be a medical practice; the method it demonstrates is industry-neutral. This page documents what's real and verified today, and is explicit about what's still in progress.

The Initial Problem

A technically strong website, invisible to AI

Dr. Chantasi, in his capacity as the practice owner rather than as an SEO consultant, wanted to understand whether patients searching for regenerative medicine, pain management, or aesthetic care in Orlando would actually find PrimeCell — including through the AI tools patients increasingly use to ask "who's the best X near me" instead of running a traditional search. The practice had invested in a conventional, well-built website and had real, verified patient reviews. The open question was whether that traditional foundation was translating into visibility inside AI-generated answers.

Methodology note
The first attempt at this test was run on a personal, logged-in ChatGPT account with memory and chat history enabled, and produced a self-aware response that referenced the tester's own business by name unprompted — a clear sign the result was contaminated by account personalization and not representative of what an actual prospective patient would see. That result was discarded. All results below were run from a signed-out ChatGPT session and a Google search on a fresh query, to approximate what a real prospective patient would encounter.

Was PrimeCell mentioned?

No, in 5 of 5 clean tests. PrimeCell was not named in the ChatGPT regenerative/pain, hormone therapy, GLP-1 weight loss, or neuropathy answers, and was not named in Google's AI Overview for the regenerative/pain query — despite ranking #1 in Google's ordinary local Places pack for that same search (5.0★, 12 reviews). The gap is specific to AI-generated answers, not general local search.

Inaccurate or missing information

No factual inaccuracies found in the few places PrimeCell does appear — Google's local Places result correctly shows PrimeCell Regenerative & Aesthetic Medicine, 5.0★ (12 reviews), correct address and phone. The issue found is absence from AI-generated answers, not misrepresentation within them.

Documented

Technical & AI-crawlability findings

primecellmed.com's robots.txt does not block AI crawlers — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, anthropic-ai, Amazonbot, Bytespider, CCBot, and others are explicitly listed and given the same access as everything else (only admin/search/config paths are disallowed, for all crawlers). Crawlability is not the barrier.

Entity & citation findings

Structured data (JSON-LD) exists for the practice and for Dr. Chantasi individually, including medical specialties (Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation, Pain Medicine) and a priceRange field. Correction (2026-08-12): an earlier version of this page incorrectly stated the business-level schema had zero sameAs links — on closer inspection it actually has 5 (Google Business Profile, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Reddit). Monitoring Process (Defined, Not Yet Run)

The plan, ahead of the results

The intended approach — even in advance of specific results — is a monthly re-test of the same core prompt set across the primary AI platforms in scope, run under comparable location context each time, with every result logged in full regardless of outcome. Any content, schema, or directory changes made during a given month will be timestamped so shifts in AI mentions can be roughly correlated with specific actions — while being honest that AI platforms are opaque, frequently updated systems, and causation is difficult to prove with confidence even when correlation is observed. This process has been defined but has not yet produced a full monitoring cycle.

Limitations

Read this case study with its limits in mind

Single case, single practice

One physician-owned practice in one metro area can't establish that this approach generalizes across industries, sizes, or markets.

Founder's own business

Kenny Chantasi has a financial interest in both companies, so this is a self-case-study — an obvious conflict of interest, disclosed rather than glossed over. It is not an independent result.

AI answers are volatile

The same prompt run twice, minutes apart, can return different answers. Any before/after comparison is a snapshot, not a controlled experiment.

Attribution is fuzzy

Connecting a specific booked appointment to a specific AI recommendation is difficult; most attribution here will be directional, not definitively causal.

Regulated industry constraints

As a medical practice, PrimeCell's public claims must stay consistent with medical advertising regulations — a constraint that won't transfer to most AnswerFoundry clients, who work in law, home services, finance, real estate, and other categories.

Methodology still being finalized

Testing cadence, prompt list, and platform list are defined in principle but haven't been run through a full cycle yet, and may be refined based on what the first rounds reveal.
